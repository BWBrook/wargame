# Comparative synthesis: command depth with limited handling

19 September 2026. One bounded synthesis of the thirteen existing case dossiers and
Barry's [fireside discussion](../fireside-discussion.md), informing the
[first-playable brief](first-playable.md). No new literature search or independent
historical verification was conducted for this synthesis.

## Evidence and conclusion

Barry's recollections establish his own preferences; the dossiers document game
mechanisms, attributed reception and researcher interpretations. Neither selected
reviews nor decades of community survival establish representative retention or
causal effects. Editions and platforms remain those specified in each dossier;
its accompanying source ledger preserves the underlying references.

The strongest working conclusion is not “make a simple wargame.” It is **make the
military decision understandable, and let its consequences interact**. The first
exercise should test commitment, geography, preservation and support together, with
few pieces and clear reports. More administrative systems would not strengthen
that initial test.

## What each case contributes—and cautions against

The right-hand column is this synthesis's design inference, not a finding that the
original game's feature caused Barry's enjoyment.

## Contradictions that must remain visible

- **Puzzle mastery versus command immersion.** Barry enjoyed solving *Unity of
  Command* yet explicitly chose qualitative advice and uncertainty (Q5–Q11).
  Rejecting exposed probability tables is not rejecting causal clarity. Reports
  should explain observed results without providing omniscience.
- **Depth versus short handling time.** The rich phases of *War of the Lance* were
  attractive to Barry, while administration exhausted other players. Q1–Q4 and
  Q18–Q20 put the player in military command with delegated support; making a
  coalition council the dominant loop would reverse that emphasis.
- **Risk versus earned advantage.** Barry wants both imperfect intelligence and
  stochastic combat (Q5), but not frequent catastrophic reversals of overwhelming
  preparation (Q6). Bound variation, distinguish uncertainty from disadvantage,
  and test multiple seeds; do not promise certainty from qualitative advice.
- **Preservation versus attachment.** The Panzer lineage and *Wesnoth* show both
  investment and reload pressure. Q15–Q17 favour ordinary formations with only
  occasional exceptional identities, and Q22 postpones linked campaigns.
- **Discrete field control versus surviving fortifications.** Q10 normally resolves
  hostile entry by battle; Q41 permits a garrison to remain after field defeat.
  Omitting sieges in the exercise is a scope cut, not a resolution against Q41.
- **An interesting opponent versus a huge AI programme.** Fixed opponents are a
  recurring ceiling, but large-scale self-play cannot establish human legibility
  or enjoyment. A small heuristic opponent and early browser play are useful
  evidence before advanced search or external models.
- **Few inputs versus too little choice.** The exercise can be fast because it has
  few formations, not because it achieves the eventual pacing target. If every
  opening has one obvious order, adding chores will not create depth.

## Superseded direction and deferred scope

Q35 selects ancient Greece within a 500–400 BC scenario frame, superseding the
initial fantasy-first framing. Q42's endorsed historical direction replaces the
earlier good-versus-bad coalition distinction with interests and obligations;
fantasy heroes and quests are parked, not silently converted into Greek mechanics.
Q44 selects 480 BC Greek coalition defence, superseding the tentative Peloponnesian
War opening. Persia is the active invading opponent here, not the abstract,
initially unallied background power discussed for a different scenario at Q39.
The first large scenario need not implement the whole century or the entire
Mediterranean map. Q38 transport, Q41 sieges and broader investment systems remain
requirements/directions for later coverage, not promises of this first exercise.

Q45 resolves the historical-outcome question: credible openings and constraints,
not obligatory Thermopylae or Salamis outcomes. It does not yet determine victory
thresholds, historical turn duration, force strengths or exact boundaries. Synthetic
prototype defaults must be labelled as such until historical scenario work and
playtesting support them.

## Decision and stopping point

Proceed with the bounded first-playable brief. This synthesis supports a testable
direction, not proof that the combination will be fun. The next evidence should be
a working command loop and a play trace, followed by a focused Fable critique and
Barry's hands-on response. Return to wider research only for a concrete historical
or design question exposed by that work; do not keep collecting attractive systems.
