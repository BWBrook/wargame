# Sources: *Warlords* and *Warlords II*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** Strategic Studies Group's 1990 *Warlords* and original 1993 IBM/DOS *Warlords II*  
**Later evidence:** The 1994 Macintosh conversion and Scenario Builder, 1995 *Warlords II Deluxe*, fan archives, tournaments, preservation objects, and current commercial bundles are used only to establish afterlife and retention. They are not silently substituted for base-game features.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original-period manual, software or museum object, direct designer/developer statement, publisher magazine, or contemporary product documentation.
- **Near-primary:** institutional collection record, faithful package archive, or direct contemporary community testimony.
- **Secondary:** independent contemporary review, later critical reconstruction, interview, or historical analysis.
- **Tertiary:** database or bibliographic orientation; not sole support for a central claim when a stronger source exists.
- **Publisher/community claim:** evidence of stated intent, perception, practice, or custodianship, but potentially promotional, anecdotal, selective, or survivor-biased.

The two manuals were downloaded as PDFs, rendered page by page, and visually inspected. The *Warlords* PDF has a useful text layer derived from a manual transcription; the *Warlords II* scan is image-backed and its OCR contains occasional punctuation and character errors. Credits, setup options, victory rules, scenario boundaries, and other central claims were checked against rendered pages rather than accepted from OCR alone. The relevant *Computer Gaming World* review page was also rendered and inspected.

## Original games and manuals

### S01 — *Warlords* manual (1990)

- Type: original common manual for all versions; **primary**.
- URLs: [PDF scan](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/wp-content/uploads/Warlords-Manual-www.lemonamiga1.pdf); [Play It Again game page](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/games/warlords/index.html)
- Used for: eight-player structure; Illuria, its eight empires, and eighty cities; complete-conquest objective; real-game Elvallie tutorial; one-to-eight human/computer mixing; Knight, Baron, Lord, and Warlord AI; equal-rules and no-secret-bonus statement; turn sequence; screen layout; city income, distributed-treasury loss, production, upkeep, disbanding, defence, towers, and razing; local army choices and vectoring; movement; sixteen army types; eight-unit stacks; navies; sequential two-hit combat; city defence by up to thirty-two armies; bonuses; heroes, ruins, artefacts, allies, and blessings; AI attitudes; conditional solo surrender; and complete credits.
- Important correction: the formal objective is all eighty cities. More than forty cities merely permits a conditional surrender offer in a one-human-versus-seven-computers game when the strongest computer is also sufficiently far behind. The player may refuse.
- Caveat: computer-specific installation, controls, display, and performance were delegated to separate cards not included here. Humorous lore and SSG's superlative description of its AI are not treated as independent evaluation.

### S02 — *Warlords II* IBM/DOS manual (1993)

- Type: original 64-page manual and scanned software documentation; **primary**.
- URLs: [archival game page](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/warlords-ii-227); [manual download](https://www.myabandonware.com/download/lvdk-warlords-ii)
- Used for: 1993 copyright; IBM/DOS and 386-class requirements; complete design and production credits; tutorial; scenario and random-map setup; side inclusion, human control, AI levels, named personalities, and fair-rule claim; Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, and custom option setup; persistent paths and automatic water movement; stack palette and interface; city economy, production investment, vectors, planted standards, and conquest choices; twenty-eight army types; eight-unit stacks and thirty-two-unit city defence; fight order; explicit combat algorithm; medals; hero levels, experience, hiring, searching, quests, and artefacts; diplomacy and credibility; Enhanced Production as an explicit handicap; conditional surrender; save facilities; and base-game hot-seat multiplayer.
- Important boundary: the manual contains a random-map generator and the ability to save generated maps, but no scenario editor, email play, modem play, or network play. Those are later-product features.
- Caveat: the scan's OCR is noisy, so numerical and boundary claims were checked visually. The manual is promotional about its AI and does not reveal the source code, search method, or empirical results.

### S03 — Play It Again, *Warlords* game record and retrospective history

- Type: Australian institutional digital-game history assembled from software objects and creator material; **near-primary / secondary**.
- URL: [Game record](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/games/warlords/index.html)
- Used for: original Amiga and IBM/DOS identity; Australian SSG context; physical components and screenshots; creator recollections; Fawkner's stated influence from *Dragons of Glory*, *Empire*, and *The Lords of Midnight*; and player comments describing strong attachment and hot-seat play.
- Caveat: the page combines archival evidence, retrospective prose, database-like dates, and public comments. It incorrectly folds later editor capability into a broad *Warlords II* description, so the 1993 manual controls that feature boundary. Comments are anecdotal and survivor-biased.

## Development and publisher evidence

### S04 — Play It Again interview/profile: Steve Fawkner

- Type: direct creator interview and institutional profile; **primary for recollection / secondary for historical framing**.
- URL: [Steve Fawkner creator page](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/creators/steve-fawkner-2/index.html)
- Used for: Fawkner's early game-making; submission through Andrew Buttery; SSG's initial reluctance toward fantasy; Ian Trout's son's role in rescuing the submission; roughly six months of refinement; telephone-and-floppy collaboration; Roger Keating's mentorship and AI contribution; 1990 publication; tens-of-thousands sales recollection; formation of Infinite Interactive; and Fawkner's description of the team effort.
- Caveat: retrospective memory recorded decades later. Sales are not audited, and the colourful discovery story is retained as the creator's account rather than independently documented fact.

### S05 — Gregor Whiley, “Warlords II Designers Notes,” *Run 5* 19

- Type: contemporary developer account in SSG's house magazine; **primary for design intent and implementation history / publisher claim for quality**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/wp-content/uploads/SSG-Run-5-Issue-19.pdf)
- Used for: the original as SSG bestseller; sequel's clean-slate rewrite and disposal of most old code; move from 640×200 to 640×480; hardware and memory choices; replacement pathfinding; persistent multi-turn destinations; recalculation as hidden terrain appears; the relationship between AI, database, and movement; claimed major AI improvement; absence of designed personalities in the original and addition of named personalities; main-screen stack palette, contextual cursors, shortcuts, and configurable buttons; multiple scenarios and random maps; production investment; diplomacy, quests, and options; simplification and direct display of bonuses; repair of AI water movement; right-click help; music; hero levels, medals, names, signposts, and saveable random maps; and collective authorship.
- Caveat: written to promote a newly released SSG product. Statements about what was rewritten and why are strong; superlatives about quality, guaranteed challenge, and enjoyment are not independent reception evidence.

### S06 — *Run 5* 20, launch response and reader letters

- Type: contemporary publisher magazine; **primary for SSG's own report / publisher and community claim**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/wp-content/uploads/SSG-Run-5-Issue-20.pdf)
- Used for: SSG's report of overwhelming response; strong Origins sales and onsite disk duplication; confirmation that the IBM sequel was newly released; distribution of the original Illuria converted to sequel format; favourable user reaction to presentation; and one reader's complaint that there were too many options, with the editor's mix-and-match response.
- Caveat: sales are unquantified and reported by the publisher. Published letters are selected, not a representative survey.

### S07 — *Run 5* 22, *Warlords II Scenario Builder* release

- Type: contemporary publisher magazine and catalogue; **primary for product separation / publisher claim for popularity**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/wp-content/uploads/SSG-Run-5-Issue-22.pdf)
- Used for: separate 1994 IBM Scenario Builder product, its commercial availability, and SSG's report that it was proving popular.
- Caveat: the popularity claim has no unit sales or user count. The source proves the editor was a follow-on product rather than a base 1993 feature.

### S08 — *Run 5* 23, Macintosh *Warlords II*

- Type: contemporary publisher magazine and catalogue; **primary for release state**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://ourdigitalheritage.org/hostedArchives/playitagain/wp-content/uploads/SSG-Run-5-Issue-23.pdf)
- Used for: evidence that the Macintosh conversion had just been released and was commercially listed in 1994.
- Caveat: does not establish an exact retail day or mechanical parity with the IBM release.

## Contemporary reception

### S09 — Alan Emrich, “The Game Which Would Be King,” *Computer Gaming World* 81, April 1991

- Type: contemporary independent review; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_81.pdf)
- Used for: description as a strategically pure fantasy war; comparison with *Empire* and *Reach for the Stars*; praise for the eight-sided contest, fixed-map positional variety, simple economy and combat, competent ruthless AI, common rules, attacks on weak positions, key-city defence, deep penetrations, razing, and attitude behaviour; plus reported sound and menu bugs and a minor map omission.
- Important correction: the review says the game ends at forty-one cities. The original manual's conditional surrender rule is more precise and controls the dossier.
- Caveat: one highly favourable review. Behavioural observations are valuable contemporary evidence; its judgement of quality and inferred influence are criticism, not telemetry.

### S10 — *Computer Gaming World* 88, November 1991 Wargame of the Year award

- Type: contemporary editorial award record; **primary for the magazine's decision / secondary for evaluation**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_88.pdf)
- Used for: *Warlords* as co-winner with *Command HQ*; editorial praise for computer intelligence, multiplayer, and the use of fantasy to bring a wargame structure to a broader audience.
- Caveat: an editorial award by one publication, not a player poll or retention measure.

### S11 — Amiga Reviews archive, contemporary *Warlords* reviews

- Type: transcriptions and indexed extracts from contemporary Amiga magazines; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Review archive](https://amigareviews.leveluphost.com/warlords.htm)
- Used for: Amiga Computing's expectation of days rather than hours; CU Amiga's “addictive and highly playable” assessment and favourable playability/lastability ratings; and Amiga Joker's contrary view that the game was simple and playable but too weak a challenge for experienced wargamers.
- Caveat: an enthusiast-hosted review archive rather than original page scans in this pass. Percentages are editorial opinions and do not establish population-wide retention. Amiga presentation and performance are not projected onto DOS.

### S12 — Contemporary Usenet discussion of *Warlords* (July 1991)

- Type: contemporaneous player review and reply; **near-primary community testimony**.
- URL: [Google Groups thread](https://groups.google.com/g/comp.sys.amiga.games/c/N7-Tky1ceQM)
- Used for: one player's highly favourable report after roughly ten to fourteen hours; description of reaching more than half the cities; recommendation; and a reply calling it very addictive while criticising the weak end/surrender indication.
- Caveat: two self-selected Amiga users, not representative evidence. The posts are most useful for session texture and the fact that even an enthusiastic player found the ending unclear.

### S13 — Ed Dille, “Art Thou A Worthy Opponent?”, *Computer Gaming World* 112, November 1993

- Type: contemporary independent *Warlords II* review; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_112.pdf)
- Used for: map-size and campaign discussion; AI levels and behavioural constraints; diplomacy and credibility; Military Advisor; capture choices; unit and bonus combinations; fifteen-to-twenty-hour full-campaign estimate; praise for sound and exceptionally strong AI that could win; and criticism of the brief victory presentation.
- Caveat: one very favourable review. Its labels for diplomatic states differ from the manual's exact Allied/Neutral/Hostile terminology, so the manual controls. The duration is an estimate, not measured telemetry.

### S14 — Graham Kinsey, *Warlords II* review, *Compute!* 161, February 1994

- Type: contemporary independent review; **secondary**.
- URLs: [issue index](https://www.atarimagazines.com/compute/index/index.php?issue=issue161); [issue PDF](https://www.commodore.ca/gallery/magazines/compute/Compute-161.pdf)
- Used for: recognition of the conquest loop, heroes, ruins, army combinations, and many setup options; criticism that the base game had no map editor, too few supplied scenarios, and insufficient random-map controls; and a report of about a minute between turns on the reviewer's 486SX.
- Caveat: the host blocked direct PDF retrieval during this pass, but the search-indexed page text exposed the relevant review. The review also appears to undercount group capacity; it is not used for stack rules. The machine-wait figure is one hardware configuration and game state.

## Fan analysis, later criticism, and realised community use

### S15 — Robert Heeter's *Warlords II* archive and FAQ

- Type: long-running fan archive begun in 1994; **secondary / community practice evidence**.
- URLs: [archive home](https://www.heeter.net/warlords/); [FAQ](https://www.heeter.net/warlords/FAQ.html); [basic game description](https://www.heeter.net/warlords/about_warlords.html)
- Used for: advanced rules, probability tools, tactics, scenarios, utilities, PBEM, league and tournament infrastructure; random-map strengths and occasional defects; reported replay of Illuria; difficulty adjustments using personalities, “I am the Greatest,” Enhanced Production, Intense Combat, and house rules; warnings that hidden maps and some razing patterns can disadvantage the AI; and evidence of sustained technical custodianship into the 2000s.
- Caveat: the archive often discusses Scenario Builder and *Deluxe* alongside the base sequel. Product boundaries were checked elsewhere. Advice reflects expert community experience and house conventions, not audited balance or typical play.

### S16 — Heeter scenario summary table

- Type: community reference table; **secondary**.
- URL: [scenario list](https://www.heeter.net/warlords/info/deluxe/deluxescen.html)
- Used for: names and retained specifications of the six original scenarios: Tutorial (2 sides, 6 cities, 6 ruins), Isles of Sorcery (4/20/16), Dragon Realms (5/40/24), and Erythea, Hadesha, and Isladia (each 8/80/40).
- Caveat: presented in a later *Deluxe* archive. It is used only where the listed original scenarios agree with the base manual's map-size categories; later scenarios are excluded.

### S17 — International *Warlords II* tournament archive

- Type: organiser-maintained community record; **primary for that organised activity / community claim for participant count**.
- URL: [tournament archive](https://www.heeter.net/warlords/tournament/)
- Used for: tournament beginning in May 1997; rounds continuing into 2001; more than 250 entrants from more than twenty-five countries; SSG-provided prizes; and concrete evidence of sustained competitive PBEM play.
- Caveat: the tournament depends on later remote-play infrastructure and enthusiast recruitment. It is evidence for the extended *Warlords II* ecosystem, not the 1993 base product or mass-market retention.

### S18 — The CRPG Addict, *Warlords* retrospective (2021)

- Type: detailed later playthrough and criticism; **secondary / anecdotal**.
- URL: [retrospective](https://crpgaddict.blogspot.com/2021/07/game-420-warlords-1990.html)
- Used for: a complete later solo-play account; report of no immediately obvious repetitive AI exploit; approximately eleven-hour experience; and criticism of terrain delays and weak empire-wide production/vector inspection and city cycling.
- Caveat: one modern player using emulation and approaching the title from a CRPG-review project. Interface observations are useful, but duration, taste, and AI experience are not representative of period users.

### S19 — The CRPG Addict, *Warlords II* retrospective (2023)

- Type: detailed later playthrough and criticism; **secondary / anecdotal**.
- URL: [retrospective](https://crpgaddict.blogspot.com/2023/02/game-484-warlords-ii-1993.html)
- Used for: scenario-name orientation; confirmation that the base game is hot seat rather than networked; observations about snowballing, upkeep and casualties, hero handling by AI, and the limits of economic depth; and one player's judgement that a game can become effectively lost before formal defeat.
- Caveat: one modern player and not a genre-pure review. Some historical and bestseller statements are retrospective and were not used without stronger support.

### S20 — MobyGames player reviews of *Warlords II*

- Type: later user reviews; **secondary / anecdotal reception**.
- URL: [player-review page](https://www.mobygames.com/game/451/warlords-ii/reviews/)
- Used for: one long-term player's praise for unit variety, music, heroes, bonuses, scenarios, terrain combinations, AI, addictiveness, and repeated returns; plus complaints about apparently extreme combat upsets, diplomacy and reputation, and inability to combine allied stacks.
- Caveat: self-selected reviews written years after release. They demonstrate possible long-term attachment and friction, not prevalence; memory may blend patches or later familiarity.

## Known but not fully examined

- No exact day-level ship date was located for either 1990 platform version of *Warlords*. The year, developer, publisher, credits, and Amiga/IBM identity are well supported.
- The first *Warlords* Macintosh conversion date is inconsistent across contemporary publisher material and later databases. Because Barry has not identified a platform and the user-defined pair is 1990/1993, the uncertainty is preserved rather than forced into a false precision.
- The original platform-specific *Warlords* reference cards were not all collected. The common mechanics are secure, while per-platform input, graphics, loading, and performance remain open.
- The *Compute!* host returned an access error for the full PDF during this pass. Search-indexed issue text exposed the relevant page, and only clearly recoverable claims are used.
- The *Warlords II* preview in *Computer Gaming World* 110 and its later strategy articles were located but were not needed once the manual, developer notes, and full review saturated the main design questions.
- *Warlords II* was a runner-up in *Computer Gaming World*'s 1994 wargame award and appears in later best-game lists, but those later accolades add little to the better-evidenced reception analysis and are not central claims here.
- No complete source code, AI architecture document, controlled AI tournament, player telemetry, completion record, systematic review corpus, audited sales ledger, or retention dataset was found.
- No attempt was made to reconstruct every Scenario Builder or *Deluxe* scenario, graphic set, utility, patch, or PBEM house rule. They establish afterlife, not the base 1993 design.
- Current store ratings are bundle-level and were not used as evidence that either original title retained typical players.
- The later branch of the series was deliberately excluded at the user's instruction.
