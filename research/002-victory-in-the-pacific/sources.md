# Sources: *Victory in the Pacific*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** Avalon Hill's 1977 first edition  
**Later evidence:** The 1981 second edition, strategy literature, variants, reviews, and organised play are used to diagnose durability, ambiguity, and balance—not silently substituted for the original game.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original rules, direct designer statement, publisher record, or official award/event result.
- **Near-primary:** faithful hosted rules text, in-house contemporary history, or an archive approved by the designer/publisher.
- **Secondary:** independent review, strategy article, retrospective, or community record.
- **Tertiary:** orientation source aggregating other accounts; not sole support for central claims where stronger evidence is available.
- **Publisher/community claim:** useful evidence of perception or practice, but potentially promotional or survivor-biased.

## Identity, rules, and design history

### S01 — BoardGameGeek, first-edition record and contemporary catalogue description

- Type: community database reproducing first-edition metadata and catalogue copy; **secondary / publisher claim**.
- URL: [First edition](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgameversion/40417/first-edition)
- Used for: Avalon Hill publication in 1977; product code 715; designer and artist credits; advertised components, eight-page rules, map size, counter count, duration, and introductory positioning.
- Caveat: the descriptive copy is promotional and the page is not an archival scan of the complete physical edition.

### S02 — Avalon Hill, *Victory in the Pacific* first-edition rules (1977)

- Type: hosted transcription of the original rulebook; **near-primary for 1977 rules**.
- URL: [First-edition rules transcription](https://www.houseofgames.ca/Rules/Victory_in_the_Pacific.html)
- Used for: original sequence and procedures; points and control; patrol and raid assignments; movement; day/night combat; air raids; retreats; bases, ports, land-based air, amphibious units, and first-turn cases; production credits.
- Caveat: this is a third-party HTML transcription rather than a photographed or publisher-hosted scan. Formatting may not perfectly reproduce the printed rulebook.

### S03 — Avalon Hill, *Victory in the Pacific* second-edition rules (1981)

- Type: hosted transcription of the revised rules; **near-primary for the 1981 edition**.
- URL: [Second-edition rules](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_format/vitp_rules.htm)
- Used for: clear reconstruction of the common system; explicit statement of the intended simplicity/abstraction; 13 sea areas, eight turns, Points of Control, staged movement, control requirements, combat, pursuit, repairs, invasions, and fleet restrictions.
- Caveat: four years later than the primary object. A procedure is not attributed to 1977 solely because it appears here.

### S04 — Richard Hamblen, “Nuts, Bolts, Philosophy and Design,” *The General* 14(4), November–December 1977, pp. 3–10

- Type: direct contemporary designer account; **primary**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2014%20No%204.pdf)
- Used for: the theatre-scale design problem; the approximately 33-area prototype; lineage from *War at Sea*; strategic rather than tactical design parameter; Japanese and Allied imperatives; codebreaking rationale for Allied second move; selection of ships, land-based air, amphibious units, bases, day/night action, and the late-1944 endpoint.
- Caveat: explains authorial intent and the publisher's presentation, not independent player reception.

### S05 — Avalon Hill editorial/development account, *The General* 14(2), 1977

- Type: contemporary in-house account; **near-primary**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2014%20No%202.pdf)
- Used for: the game's expansion from an intended *War at Sea* magazine variant into a separate boxed product; map and counter growth; low initial publicity and later visible-play interest; notice that rule glitches would be revised.
- Caveat: publisher history and marketing commentary.

### S06 — “An Interview with Richard Hamblen,” *The General* 16(5), 1980

- Type: direct designer/developer interview; **primary opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2016%20No%205.pdf)
- Used for: confirmation that Hamblen based *Victory in the Pacific* on *War at Sea* and for background on his preference for balancing playability, strategic direction, and historical effect.
- Caveat: retrospective and published by Avalon Hill.

### S07 — Glenn Petroski, *Victory in the Pacific* Questions and Answers / edition history (1997 compilation)

- Type: community compilation whose answers were submitted to and approved by Richard Hamblen or Avalon Hill; **near-primary clarification record**.
- URL: [Approved Q&A archive](https://grognard.com/errata/victpac.txt)
- Used for: December 1981 second-edition date; identifying marks; unchanged map/counters; meaningful rule differences; air-raid, amphibious, optional, and first-turn clarifications; evidence of the long rules-interpretation tail.
- Caveat: compiled twenty years after first publication. Approval gives it authority as clarification, but it is not the original 1977 text.

### S08 — Charles S. Roberts Awards history

- Type: official award record; **primary**.
- URL: [Awards history](https://charlessrobertsawards.com/history/)
- Used for: *Victory in the Pacific* as the 1977 Best Strategic Game, credited to Richard Hamblen and Avalon Hill.

## Reception and retrospective assessment

### S09 — Published reception references assembled in the general overview

- Type: **tertiary orientation**, with bibliographic pointers to contemporary books.
- URL: [Overview and reception bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_the_Pacific)
- Underlying works identified:
  - Jon Freeman, *The Complete Book of Wargames* (1980), pp. 188–189.
  - Nicholas Palmer, *The Best of Board Wargaming* (1980), pp. 35–36 and 166–167.
  - Martin Campion, *The Guide to Simulations/Games for Education and Training* (1980).
- Used for: cautious reporting of early judgements that the game was very abstract but strategically instructive, exciting and accessible; contemporary concern about combat tedium, unrealistic return-to-port movement, and side balance.
- Caveat: the complete original book pages were not inspected during this pass. These assessments are not used alone for a central claim and are explicitly reported rather than treated as verified quotations.

### S10 — Ray Freeman, “Victory in the Pacific — a review” (circa 1997)

- Type: long-time player's review; **secondary, tournament-oriented**.
- URL: [Grognard.com review](https://grognard.com/reviews/vitp.txt)
- Used for: three-to-five-hour duration; high interaction; comparison with *War at Sea*; patrol/raid, perimeter, port and base decision structure; low rules complexity but substantial mastery; strategy-article volume; small expert Japanese edge and Points-of-Control bidding; active repeat-play culture.
- Caveat: strongly favourable enthusiast perspective and claims about uniqueness or the player base are not independently universal.

### S11 — RockyMountainNavy, “Retrospective — Victory in the Pacific” (2017)

- Type: detailed modern retrospective; **secondary**.
- URL: [Retrospective review](https://rockymountainnavy.com/2017/03/08/wargamewednesday-retrospective-victory-in-the-pacific-avalon-hill-1977-second-edition/)
- Used for: praise of the central mechanics and historical arc; criticism of dense rule organisation, dated map/counters, stacking space, repetitive dice procedure, inconsistent speed-roll intuition, scripted Turn One, perfect reinforcement information, and limited production agency.
- Caveat: assesses the 1981 rules and a modern reader's usability expectations.

### S12 — Marc Michalik, “Tiny Little Branches: Victory in the Pacific,” *Game Developer* (2016)

- Type: designer/enthusiast essay; **secondary opinion**.
- URL: [Design essay](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tiny-little-branches-victory-in-the-pacific)
- Used for: praise of the game as a strategic theatre-command and brinkmanship system; the view that its simple area structure captures naval behaviour unlike a land-front game.
- Caveat: advocacy rather than systematic review.

## Strategy, balance, and analytical culture

### S13 — *The General* cumulative index, volumes 1–20

- Type: magazine bibliographic index; **primary for publication history**.
- URL: [Index PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/z02%20The%20General%20Index%20Vol%201%20to%2020.pdf)
- Used for: evidence of the breadth of published material—design history, replays, Japanese and Allied strategy, air power, opening systems, realism variants, British fleet analysis, postal play, and Q&A.
- Caveat: an index proves publication, not the validity of each article's strategic conclusion.

### S14 — Alan R. Moon, “TKO in Three,” *The General* 17(4), 1980

- Type: competitive strategy article; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2017%20No%204.pdf)
- Used for: the prominent claim that a Japanese opening could come close to guaranteeing victory; acknowledgement that some players saw the game as too simple or luck-driven; evidence of developed opening theory.
- Caveat: a provocative strategy claim, not a settled balance result.

### S15 — Charles Lutz, “Pearl Harbor Defended,” *The General* 18(2), 1981

- Type: competitive response and appraisal; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2018%20No%202.pdf)
- Used for: a response to deterministic Japanese-opening claims; praise for playability, broad strategy, enjoyable tactics, varied approaches, and balance; criticism that the full campaign could still run long.
- Caveat: expert counter-analysis within an Avalon Hill magazine.

### S16 — “TKO in Two,” *The General* 22(2), 1985

- Type: competitive strategy article; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2022%20No%202.pdf)
- Used for: the contrary claim that aggressive play gave the United States an enormous advantage; evidence that opening theory generated counter-theory rather than consensus.
- Caveat: deliberately strong strategic thesis; title and argument respond to earlier literature.

### S17 — Alan Applebaum, “Keep on Truk-in,” *The General* 25(4), 1989

- Type: long-horizon strategy analysis; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2025%20No%204.pdf)
- Used for: argument that the game is balanced and predominantly skill-driven across all eight turns; criticism of overconcentration on the opening; evidence of multi-turn lookahead and perimeter theory.
- Caveat: expert assessment rather than statistical balance study.

### S18 — Gameaholics and community article/tournament archives

- Type: curated fan and tournament resources; **secondary / community record**.
- URLs: [Article index](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_articles/vitp_article_index.htm); [Tournament hub](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_tournament.htm); [fan overview](https://www.kobudovenlo.nl/victoryinthepacific/)
- Used for: the range of original and archived strategies, replays, tournament adjustments, variants, scenarios, teaching material, digital-play aids, and the “Yahtzee in the Pacific” nickname; evidence that players saw meaningful admiral-level problems beneath simple dice resolution.
- Caveat: survivor-biased enthusiast curation; pages may combine several rules conventions.

### S19 — “The Odds in Victory in the Pacific”

- Type: player-created probability and tactical analysis; **secondary**.
- URL: [Odds article](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_articles/vitp_odds_article.htm)
- Used for: detailed day/night, gunnery, airstrike, and target-allocation probabilities; evidence that experienced players treated luck as calculable risk rather than pure chaos.
- Caveat: calculations apply to specified assumptions and do not determine strategic value by themselves.

### S20 — “Battles of Numbers”: Markov-chain combat analysis

- Type: mathematical fan analysis; **secondary**.
- URL: [Markov analysis](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_articles/markov/battles_of_numbers.htm)
- Used for: many-to-many combat modelling, retreat thresholds, casualty versus objective value, and evidence of an optional deep analytical layer.
- Caveat: a formal model of a bounded subsystem, not proof that typical players use or enjoy that level of calculation.

### S21 — “A Normal Turn One” variant

- Type: experienced-player rules variant; **secondary diagnostic evidence**.
- URL: [Turn One variant](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_articles/turn_one.htm)
- Used for: explicit attempt to remove “location uncertain” disasters and restore Allied decisions while retaining variability and Japanese opening advantage.
- Caveat: not part of the 1977 game; its value here is evidence of the original opening's perceived friction.

## Organised play and longevity

### S22 — Boardgame Players Association, *Victory in the Pacific* event history and PBEM record

- Type: official organised-play record; **primary for event activity**.
- URLs: [Face-to-face event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/vip.html); [play-by-email event](https://boardgamers.org/pbem/pbem-vip.html)
- Used for: champion and attendance records from 1991 through 2025; 2025 field of 26; repeated play-by-email tournaments from 2000 through 2025; evidence of long-running competitive participation.
- Caveat: event attendance measures a committed niche community, not the wider number of owners or casual players. Years without a recorded event are not assigned a cause without evidence.

### S23 — Boardgame Players Association, 2003 championship report

- Type: official event report; **primary for that event, promotional in tone**.
- URL: [2003 event report](https://w.boardgamers.org/yearbook03/vippge.htm)
- Used for: 39 entrants, 75 games, teaching of newcomers, repeat play, and the reported social/camaraderie component of retention.
- Caveat: organiser narrative naturally emphasises a successful event.

### S24 — Gameaholics, tournament resources and Midwest Open

- Type: community tournament record; **secondary / organiser claim**.
- URLs: [Tournament hub](https://gameaholics.com/vitp_tournament.htm); [Midwest Open](https://gameaholics.com/conventions/vitp_con_midwest_open.htm)
- Used for: a dedicated multi-round weekend format, online brackets and conventions, scenario/bidding infrastructure, and a long-lived player network beyond the main convention championship.
- Caveat: historical participation and longevity claims are maintained by organisers and may not be continuously updated.

### S25 — Eric Pass, *Victory in the Pacific* play-by-email replay

- Type: detailed player replay and post-game commentary; **secondary**.
- URL: [PBEM replay](https://www.nyx.net/~epass/vitppbem.html)
- Used for: concrete examples of dynamic reversals, Points of Control versus fleet-loss decisions, day/night variance, strategic base choices, resignation before Turn Eight, and the very different pace of face-to-face versus email play.
- Caveat: one match and two players' interpretations; some remarks are anecdotal or humorous rather than general findings.

### S26 — “Victory in the Pacific New Edition — Interview with John Pack” (updated 2024)

- Type: interview about a prospective redevelopment; **primary for proposal, publisher/designer claim**.
- URL: [Interview](https://therewillbe.games/87910/7612-victory-in-the-pacific-new-edition-interview-with-john-pack)
- Used for: later custodians' account of continuing popularity; features they intended to preserve—named ships, area play, campaign speed, momentum swings—and intended repairs including clearer rules, shorter scenarios, balance work, and a larger map.
- Caveat: a proposal, not evidence that a new edition was completed or released. The interview's recency label may reflect page maintenance rather than a wholly new report.

### S27 — Strategic analysis of the sea areas and perimeter

- Type: experienced-player strategy essay; **secondary**.
- URL: [Strategic area analysis](https://grognard.com/strategy/vitp_st1.html)
- Used for: opening/middle/endgame framing; perimeter geometry; how bases, control, and connected areas create multi-turn routes, vulnerabilities, and opportunity costs.
- Caveat: prescriptive strategy from one analytical tradition, not neutral description.

## Known but not fully examined

- The complete original pages from Jon Freeman's *The Complete Book of Wargames*, Nicholas Palmer's *The Best of Board Wargaming*, and Martin Campion's education/training guide were not available for direct inspection in this pass. Their assessments are therefore represented only through S09 and are not quoted at length.
- Individual *The General* series replays and every strategy article listed in S13 were not all read in full. The index supports the existence and breadth of the literature; S14–S17 provide a deliberately varied sample of its arguments.
- No verified contemporary sales total was located.
- No systematic review corpus or abandonment/retention dataset was located. Community ratings were not treated as a substitute.
