# Fantasy Wargame Research Dossier

**Scope:** strategic fantasy board and computer wargames, with selected operational, tactical, adventure-strategy and low-fantasy boundary cases. Board-game emphasis runs from the 1960s campaign tradition to the present; computer emphasis runs from the early 1980s through 2006, with later grand-strategic exemplars.

**Prepared for:** a new Codex-assisted game concept whose closest stated ancestor is SSI’s *War of the Lance* (1989).

## How to read this catalogue

- **Essential / Very high / High / Medium / Low** is a design-research priority, not a quality score.
- Tactical and role-playing boundary cases are included only when they illuminate a design problem likely to arise in a strategic fantasy game.
- The 1960s produced campaign practice more than commercial fantasy board games; Tony Bath’s Hyborian campaign is therefore treated as a precursor rather than a publication.

## The central lineage

The most direct chain is **Tony Bath’s campaign practice → early army/hero/diplomacy board wargames such as *Divine Right* and *Dragon Pass* → TSR’s *Dragons of Glory* (1986) → SSI’s *War of the Lance* (1989)**. A second branch runs through *The Lords of Midnight*, *Warlords*, *Sword of Aragon*, *Master of Magic* and *Heroes of Might and Magic*. Modern board designs then contribute better approaches to dual-track victory (*War of the Ring*), asymmetry (*Root*), approachable multi-domain conquest (*Conquest of Nerath*), and current hex-and-counter campaigning (*Burning Banners*).

# 5. Design-problem matrix

| Design problem | Best exemplars | What to extract / avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral powers and recruitment** | Divine Right; Dragons of Glory; Demonlord; Greyhawk Wars; Doomdark’s Revenge | Give neutrals interests, fears, rivalries and changing thresholds. Avoid a deterministic ‘spend X diplomacy points to unlock nation’ checklist. |
| **War plus quest** | SPI War of the Ring; War of the Ring 2e; Elric; Swords & Sorcery; War in Middle Earth | Both layers must threaten and enable one another. A quest layer that merely grants bonuses becomes side content; an unstoppable quest makes armies irrelevant. |
| **Heroes within armies** | Warlords; Sword of Aragon; Gemfire; Heroes III; Brigandine | Heroes should command, scout, recruit, negotiate or quest—not simply multiply a stack until ordinary units cease to matter. |
| **Independent characters** | Lords of Midnight; Doomdark’s Revenge; Majesty; Age of Conan | Partial autonomy creates personality and surprise. Make motives legible enough that outcomes feel causal rather than random. |
| **Tactical battle layer** | Titan; Sword of Aragon; Master of Magic; Age of Wonders; Total War: Warhammer | Offer rapid auto-resolution and reserve detailed battles for pivotal engagements. Repeated tactical play can consume the campaign and expose AI weaknesses. |
| **Operational scale without micro** | Kohan; Warmaster; Songs of Silence; Dragon Force | Command companies or battle groups, expose formation and supply, and let preparation matter more than clicking speed. |
| **Magic as strategy** | Elric; Master of Magic; Dominions; Age of Wonders | Use scarce, telegraphed rule-breaking powers with counters and opportunity costs. Avoid hundreds of near-duplicate combat modifiers. |
| **Economy and logistics** | Sword of Aragon; Warrior Knights; Kohan; Burning Banners | Tie military capacity to places, roads, seasons, loyalty and replenishment rather than generic resource harvesting detached from the fiction. |
| **Faction asymmetry** | Nomad Gods; Chaos in the Old World; Root; Dominions; Bloodstones | Different strategic verbs produce identity. The costs are tutorial burden, balance workload, content production and much harder AI. |
| **Hidden information** | Beastlord; Stratego Legends; Battle for Rokugan; Wizard Kings | Fog, spies and feints create genuine strategy. Keep the state trackable and avoid excessive dummy maintenance or memory tests. |
| **Narrative world state** | King of Dragon Pass; Oath; Thea; Conquest of Elysium | Events should arise from faction, geography and prior choices, producing plausible alternate histories rather than arbitrary story cards. |
| **Multiple victory routes** | War of the Ring; Inis; Lords of Hellas; King of Dragon Pass | Routes should interact and be contestable. Hidden or abrupt victories need strong telegraphing to preserve counterplay. |
| **Pacing and anti-snowball** | Small World; Blood Rage; Oath; Britannia | Use decline, phase-specific scoring, productive losses, coalitions or escalating threats to avoid a long mopping-up endgame. |
| **Modding and content architecture** | Warlords II/III; Myth II; Battle for Wesnoth; Age of Wonders | Separate engine, data and scenario logic. A readable editor and deterministic seed/replay system will greatly amplify Codex-assisted development. |

# 6. Provisional design principles for a modern War of the Lance-like game

1. **Keep the strategic map primary.** Tactical battles should be optional, short, auto-resolvable and reserved for genuinely important engagements.
2. **Treat heroes as strategic agents, not RPG protagonists.** Command, diplomacy, scouting, recruitment, sabotage, questing and morale are more useful than inventories full of incremental equipment.
3. **Make neutral nations political actors.** Their alignments should respond to threats, leaders, history and geography; recruitment should be uncertain but intelligible.
4. **Use two or three interacting victory dimensions.** Military control, alliance legitimacy and a quest or artefact track can coexist, but each must permit counterplay.
5. **Ground the economy in the fiction.** Cities, roads, seasonal campaigning, supply, population, loyalty and replacements are preferable to generic wood/ore harvesting.
6. **Let magic break rules sparingly.** Strategic spells should be memorable, costly and counterable rather than a large catalogue of small modifiers.
7. **Design asymmetry at the level of decisions.** Factions should value different places, units, alliances and tempos—not merely receive +10% combat bonuses.
8. **Prevent the mopping-up phase.** Political collapse, escalating crises, finite campaigning seasons, exhaustion, threshold victories or a hard end date can preserve tension.
9. **Prefer companies and armies over individual-unit micro.** The player should make operational choices about composition, route, stance and commander rather than issue dozens of repetitive orders.
10. **Build for instrumentation and modding from day one.** Data-driven factions, units, events and scenarios; seeded randomness; replay logs; and headless simulations will make Codex-assisted iteration far more powerful.

# 7. Suggested dossier template for each game studied in depth

For each shortlisted title, record: **campaign scale; turn structure; map topology; force representation; hero functions; diplomacy; neutral factions; economy; supply; recruitment; magic; fog of war; battle resolution; victory conditions; anti-snowball mechanisms; AI model; interface strengths; failure modes; modding/data architecture; and one mechanic worth prototyping.**
