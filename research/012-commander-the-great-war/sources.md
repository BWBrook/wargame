# Sources: *Commander: The Great War*

Accessed 23 August 2026 unless otherwise stated. Source IDs are used in the accompanying dossier. The ledger distinguishes the original Windows 1.1.2 release of 12 November 2012 from later official patches, the Mac/iPad/Steam ports, current community evidence, and the Potzblitz modification.

## Rules, product identity, and first-party evidence

### S01 — The Lordz Games Studio / Slitherine and Matrix Games, *Commander: The Great War Game Manual* (2012)

- Type: original official manual; **primary for launch rules, interface, credits, and stated design framing**.
- URL: [Steam-hosted PDF](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/312350/manuals/CTGW-manual-EBOOK.pdf?t=1726822204).
- Local inspection: downloaded, text-extracted, rendered, and visually checked during this case. PDF metadata reports creation on 2 November 2012; the document is seventy-four pages and predates the 12 November launch.
- Used for: five campaign starts and common-map boundary; alliance choice and resource-based difficulty; 164×76 campaign map; army-scale formation handling; controls, fog of war, minimap, sprites/counters, terrain, rivers, climate, and entrenchment; PP, MP, AM, RC, TC, NM, upkeep, deficit, and convoys; move-then-attack, advance, swapping, rail, ranged attacks, unit roles, strength, efficiency, quality, Assault, Bombard, Shock, counterbattery, interception, escort, production, commanders, research, diplomacy, surrender, victory, supply, repair, upgrades, disbanding, and full credits.
- Important launch-specific rules: being attacked prevents further entrenchment; nations receive ten points of free upkeep; air and naval units are always fully supplied; disbanding can return production and manpower. Several of these changed later and must not be replaced silently by mature rules.
- Evidence caveat: statements that the game is easy to play, hard to master, and challenging even for experienced players are publisher/developer claims within a manual, not independent performance evidence.
- OCR/layout caveat: campaign/research/interface screenshots contain some information not represented perfectly in extracted text. Selected pages and a contact sheet were visually inspected before using layout-dependent claims.

### S02 — Slitherine, “Commander — The Great War Now Available!” (12 November 2012)

### S03 — Slitherine / Matrix Games, official product record

- Type: current publisher/store record; **primary for commercial identity and specification, mixed-version for features**.
- URLs: [Slitherine](https://www.slitherine.com/product/commander-the-great-war); [Matrix Games](https://www.matrixgames.com/game/commander-the-great-war).

### S04 — Armchair General, “Game Previews from Slitherine-Matrix Press Days 2012”

- Type: contemporary press-event report conveying direct developer briefings; **near-primary for development intent, secondary for interpretation**.
- URL: [Armchair General](https://armchairgeneral.com/game-previews-from-slitherine-matrix-press-days-2012-an-acg-report.htm).
- Used for: engine rewritten from scratch for portability; intended series engine; tooltips/tutorials meant to allow immediate entry; annual campaign starts; about forty fixed or conditional events; US entry partly affected by U-boat warfare; ammunition stockpiling before offensives; auto-entrenchment; rail movement; research cost; and commanders with possible negative effects.
- Caveat: pre-release details can change. The report mentions morale loss from sitting in trenches, a mechanism not stated in the launch manual; it is not attributed to the final rules without corroboration.

### S05 — Slitherine, “Research and Development Screens Released for Commander: The Great War” (24 October 2012)

- Type: pre-release publisher feature note; **primary for intended technological arc, promotional in valuation**.
- URL: [Slitherine news](https://www6.slitherine.com/news/research-and-development-screens-released-for-commander-the-great-war24-10-12).
- Used for: explicit framing of World War I as trench attrition created by weapon/tactics imbalance; tanks, aircraft, and other advances as means to restore mobility; and research versus attrition as a central player problem.
- Caveat: this describes design intent and historical framing, not proof that late-war mobility was balanced or that the simulation is accurate.

### S06 — The Lordz Games Studio developer response, “Commander: The Great War in the making — Dev Diary Episode 3” (16 December 2010)

- Type: direct development-period forum statement; **primary for AI intent, not architecture or performance**.
- URL: [Slitherine forum](https://forum.slitherine.com/viewtopic.php?t=20200).
- Used for: new engine and “brand new AI”; stated intention to react to enemy action rather than follow global preset conditions; acknowledgement that AI could make or break a strategy game.
- Critical limitation: the developer did not disclose the algorithm, search, evaluation, or final implementation. A 2010 aspiration is not evidence that the 2012 opponent consistently achieved it.

### S07 — The Lordz Games Studio, official patch changelog (versions 1.1.2–1.6.x)

- Type: developer-maintained change history; **primary for version chronology, acknowledged changes, and defect fixes**.
- URL: [Slitherine forum changelog](https://forum.slitherine.com/viewtopic.php?t=48541).
- Used for:
  - v1.1.2 initial release on 12 November 2012;
  - v1.1.4 AI-resource bug, Austria-Hungary and German openings, Entente Gallipoli, submarine construction, final-nation defence, and naval changes;
  - v1.2/v1.3 higher bomber/airship ammunition costs, fighter interception of empty-city raids, faster city recovery, entrenchment continuation after attack, and AI crash fixes;
  - v1.4/1.4.2 fleet/port/submarine changes, reduced bomber/artillery effect, PP rebalance, build-time changes, and early fighters reframed as reconnaissance;
  - v1.5.x AI speed/crash/long-loop fixes, double-click confirmation, cross-platform play, and technical improvements; and
  - v1.6.2 removal of free upkeep, national manpower/income changes, fleet and morale changes, enhanced naval AI, broader anti-air defence, reduced air effectiveness, and extension of the 1918 campaign.
- Analytical value: patch notes do not say why every number changed or quantify pre-patch severity, but repeated changes to the same relationship are strong evidence of recognised tuning pressure.
- Date caveat: the forum post header and embedded readme dates are not a simple chronological publication record. Individual version labels and dates inside the changelog control the dossier's chronology.

### S09 — Lordzimoa / Dr. Duh, pinned gameplay guide and tips (2014)

## Contemporary reception and developed play

### S10 — Slitherine review collation and Rmarsden community review (2012–14)

- Type: publisher forum preserving excerpts from external reviews plus a long community review; **secondary/community evidence**.
- URL: [Slitherine forum](https://forum.slitherine.com/viewtopic.php?t=39365).
- Used for: preserved Rock Paper Shotgun excerpt on the 118-turn first campaign and praised scale/pace/workload; “one more turn” language from another external review; Rmarsden's praise for simple handling, multi-front uncertainty, AI exploitation of mistakes, atmosphere, and attrition; and adverse findings on shallow diplomacy, hidden enemy morale, roster narrowing, weak naval play, and an underwhelming endgame/statistical summary.
- Preservation caveat: several original external links are dead or inaccessible. Short excerpts are used as attributed preserved evidence, not reconstructed full reviews. Rmarsden is a self-published forum review and not a representative sample.

### S11 — Dave Mackey, “Game Comparison / Review: Commander: The Great War and Strategic Command World War I” (19 November 2012)

- Type: launch-period comparison by a disclosed *Commander* beta tester; **secondary with material conflict-of-interest disclosure**.
- URL: [Dave Enjoys](https://daveenjoys.com/game-comparison-review-commander-the-great-war-and-strategic-command-world-war-i/).
- Used for: beautifully rendered graphics; “beer and pretzels” accessibility; many hours of play; five starts as the same map at different dates; less depth, diplomacy, historical event texture, and scenario variety than *Strategic Command*; faster turns; repair-mode convenience; end-turn event summary; and novice friendliness.
- Caveat: a beta tester can provide detailed product comparison but is not independent. His preference for lighter play is explicit, and replayability is comparative judgement rather than measured retention.

### S12 — Justin “hadberz” Taylor, GameSpot user review (14 November 2012)

- Type: launch user review by a manual-credited beta tester; **secondary with disclosed development proximity**.
- URL: [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/commander-the-great-war/user-reviews/2200-109342/).
- Used for: very favourable graphics; easy learning with strategic complexity; AI exploiting gaps; difficult front-line breakthrough; need for artillery and ammunition; simplified diplomacy/production/research; PBEM; high-resolution stability; and early praise for trench warfare.
- Caveat: Taylor appears in S01's beta-testing credits. His 10/10 review two days after release is evidence of an informed enthusiast's reaction, not independent critical consensus or repeated-play longevity.

### S13 — Matrix Games, “Impressions after a couple of days of play” (November–December 2012)

- Type: launch community discussion; **self-selected primary testimony about sessions, secondary for design quality**.

## Balance, current reception, and afterlife

### S18 — Steam product record

- Type: current publisher/store record; **primary for current availability/support notice, mutable community snapshot elsewhere**.
- URL: [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/312350?cc=gb&l=english).
- Used for: continuing Windows listing; five campaign starts; cross-platform/iPad promotional lineage; current mostly-positive review snapshot; explicit notice that the developer no longer officially supports the game, the last patch is from 2015, and glitches may remain.
- Snapshot: the live English store view showed 78% positive among 260 counted reviews on 23 August 2026. Counts, price, platform compatibility, and percentage are mutable; the review population mixes versions and cannot establish launch reception.

### S19 — Steam user-review corpus

- Type: self-selected current and historical user reviews; **community evidence, strongly version-mixed**.
- URL: [top-rated review view](https://steamcommunity.com/app/312350/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated).
- Used for: detailed combined-arms sequencing; long recorded playtimes; claims that single-player becomes dull while multiplayer endures; praise for graphics and scope; criticism of AI, naval/build behaviour, artillery, fighters, bombers, zeppelins, convoy interception, theatre allocation, and unit balance; and explicit acknowledgement that version 1.6 changed earlier balance.
- Caveat: storefront reviews are neither representative nor mutually consistent. Recorded hours strengthen evidence of developed exposure but do not make a balance claim correct. Positive and negative accounts are retained as conflict rather than averaged.

### S20 — Steam discussion, “AI cheating” / zeppelin balance (2017)

- Type: contentious community discussion after final official support; **low-to-moderate strength for exploit articulation**.
- URL: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/312350/discussions/0/152393186501930834/).
- Used for: explicit player description of zeppelins as grossly overpowered; strategic-bombing role; disagreement over counterplay and rapid conquest; and evidence that airship balance remained contested after the official patch era.
- Caveat: polemical multiplayer discussion, no controlled test, no version audit, and no representative sample. It is corroborating developed-play evidence only; the official changelog in S07 carries more weight.

### S21 — Slitherine diplomacy and balance discussion (November 2012)

## Source-condition summary

The strongest evidence is the launch manual (S01), launch/product records (S02–S03), and official changelog (S07). They establish what the game does and how materially it changed. Contemporary developer evidence (S04–S06, S08–S09) clarifies intent but cannot prove execution. Reception sources (S10–S17) converge on accessibility, attractive presentation, planned offensives, multi-front tension, and long-session pull, but several authors were beta testers or reviewed later ports. Steam and forum sources (S18–S23) are best used for developed tactics, persistent disputes, and afterlife, never as representative telemetry.

The central air-power finding is unusually robust because three evidence classes align: the original rules deliberately assign fighters an efficiency-shock role and airships strategic attack (S01); developed guides and players exploit those roles (S09, S19–S20); and official patches repeatedly change ammunition cost, interception, city recovery, and air effectiveness (S07). The exact severity in any one version remains uncertain, but the underlying tuning problem does not depend on Barry's recollection alone.
