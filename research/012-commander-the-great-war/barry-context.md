# Barry context: *Commander: The Great War*

This file quarantines information supplied by Barry before independent research. It is context for the later fireside discussion, not external evidence that any feature succeeded generally.

Barry added *Commander: The Great War* after the initially planned corpus because he remembered it as another highly influential tile-based grand-strategy game. He described it as “a beautiful game” into which he poured many hours.

He specifically recalled:

- liking the range of campaigns or scenarios set at different phases of World War I;
- the game meeting a difficult design challenge: making the “meat grinder” of trench warfare engaging;
- genuine possibilities for breakthroughs and rapid advances, including early mobile thrusts and late-war stormtroopers and combined arms;
- both the Entente and Central Powers having a real shot at victory; and
- important flaws, especially fighters being “cheesed” to destroy ground armies and airships being used to degrade production bases on a massive, grossly ahistorical scale.

Barry explicitly proposed that flaws belong in the analysis rather than disqualifying an otherwise influential game.

## Research handling

- The primary object is the original Windows release of 12 November 2012. Later patches are separated because they materially changed air power, economics, AI, naval balance, and campaign duration.
- “Tile-based” is normalised mechanically to a 164×76 **hex** map with army-scale formations.
- The five nominated scenarios are recorded using the game's own term, five **campaigns** beginning in 1914, 1915, 1916, 1917, and 1918. They share one map but start from different historical strategic states.
- Barry's “stormtrooper” recollection is investigated without presuming a literal unit. The original manual exposes no dedicated stormtrooper formation or infiltration phase; the effect appears to be abstracted through the 1918 Kaiserschlacht setup, infantry Assault improvements, artillery and air shock, armour, and advance mechanics.
- Fighter and airship mechanisms are separated into documented launch intent, developed player tactics, and official rebalance history. Barry's memory is not itself used to prove severity.
- Alliance viability is treated as a question of credible strategic paths and temporal asymmetry, not presumed numerical equality. No representative win-rate data were found.
- Potzblitz is afterlife evidence only. It is not substituted for the original game.
- No cross-game comparison, fireside interpretation, or future fantasy-game recommendation is made in this case.
