# Sources: *The Ancient Art of War*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** Evryware and Brøderbund's 1984 IBM PC/PCjr/XT release  
**Later evidence:** Macintosh, Apple II, Japanese, and late European ports; contemporary reviews; later criticism; preservation; fan documentation; and genre histories are used to test reception, longevity, and retrospective importance—not silently substituted for the original PC object's mechanics or interface.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original-period manual, reference card, software object, direct designer/developer statement, publisher record, or platform-specific documentation.
- **Near-primary:** contemporary publisher profile, museum catalogue, or faithful preservation record close to the original object.
- **Secondary:** independent contemporary review, later critical review, strategy guide, or historical analysis.
- **Tertiary:** database or bibliography used for orientation; not sole support for central claims when stronger evidence is available.
- **Publisher/community claim:** useful evidence of perception, practice, or custodianship, but potentially promotional, anecdotal, or survivor-biased.

The original IBM manual and two-page command card were rendered page by page and visually inspected. The manual is an image-only scan, so OCR was used for search and transcription but checked against the rendered pages before central mechanical claims were recorded.

## Original object, documentation, and publication identity

### S01 — *The Ancient Art of War* IBM PC manual (1984)

- Type: original manual; **primary**.
- URLs: [Manual scan](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_DOS/Manual/formated/Ancient_Art_of_War.pdf); [archival game page with manual download](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/the-ancient-art-of-war-3a)
- Used for: Dave and Barry Murry programming credit; Joanna Dougherty manual credit; 1984 copyright; IBM PC/PCjr/XT requirements; title-screen demonstration; tutorial; all eleven supplied campaigns and their ratings; twenty-squad and fourteen-person limits; troop relationships; map, terrain, sighting, food, condition, influence, fort, reinforcement, encounter, formation, tactical-order, flag, surrender, save, Game Generator, and campaign-rule systems; descriptions of the eight computer commanders; the manual's hierarchy of indirect over attritional victory; original PC interface and hardware constraints.
- Caveat: comprehensive for the intended PC rules and interface, but written partly as marketing and entertainment. Its historical essays, cultural representations, claim of more than two thousand variations, and statements about named commanders are not treated as verified history or measured replay.

### S02 — IBM PC command reference card

- Type: original packaged reference aid; **primary**.
- URLs: [Direct reference-card scan](https://www.myabandonware.com/download/lvat-the-ancient-art-of-war); [archival game page](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/the-ancient-art-of-war-3a)
- Used for: keyboard and joystick input; campaign, map, movement, squad, formation, encounter, zoom, time-rate, save, and quit commands; confirmation that save requires a data disk and returns to DOS; concise evidence of the original command burden.
- Caveat: hosted by an abandonware archive rather than the publisher. Both pages were inspected visually, and the card is used only for the original PC interface it explicitly documents.

### S03 — Media Archaeology Lab collection object

- Type: museum/laboratory catalogue record; **near-primary metadata**.
- URL: [Collection record](https://www.mediaarchaeologylab.com/collection/the-ancient-art-of-war-b80eded/)
- Used for: physical-object confirmation of *The Ancient Art of War* as a 1984 IBM PC title developed by Evryware and published by Brøderbund.
- Caveat: concise catalogue metadata rather than a release ledger or rules source; it does not establish an exact retail ship date.

### S04 — Powerhouse Collection boxed software object

- Type: museum catalogue and photographed object; **near-primary material record**.
- URL: [Collection object](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/77970)
- Used for: independent confirmation of a 1984 boxed object, the Murry brothers' design/development role, Brøderbund publication, packaging, and survival as a collected historical artefact.
- Caveat: museum cataloguing can inherit donor or database metadata and does not resolve month-level release chronology.

### S05 — *Computer Gaming World* 20, January–February 1985 preview

- Type: launch-period trade/enthusiast-magazine preview; **primary for contemporary availability and positioning**.
- URLs: [Issue PDF](https://mirrors.apple2.org.za/ftp.apple.asimov.net/documentation/magazines/computer_gaming_world/Computer%20Gaming%20World-1985_0102_issue20.pdf); [issue index](https://www.retromags.com/magazines/usa/computer-gaming-world/computer-gaming-world-issue-20/)
- Used for: contemporary listing for the IBM PC/XT/PCjr, $44.95 price, and evidence that the product's first documented press identity was IBM rather than a simultaneous Apple II release.
- Caveat: a short preview, not a review or proof of the first day of sale. Price and platform describe that listing only.

## Development and declared intent

### S06 — Russell Sipe, “IBM Goes to War,” *Computer Gaming World* 21, April–May 1985

- Type: contemporary independent review containing a direct Dave Murry statement; **primary for quoted design intent / secondary for evaluation**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://mirrors.apple2.org.za/ftp.apple.asimov.net/documentation/magazines/computer_gaming_world/Computer%20Gaming%20World-1985_0405_issue21.pdf)
- Used for: the brothers' intention to reach the general public, combine action with strategy, and privilege creative strategy over brute force; Brøderbund's strategy-game positioning; eleven campaigns; tactical zoom; scenario construction; Sipe's strong praise; Game Generator enthusiasm; missing human-versus-human play; inability to reproduce artillery-era battles; and his judgement that the title was a genuine wargame despite its broader label.
- Caveat: one highly favourable review. The direct statement is strong evidence of intent, while Sipe's “ideal” and “ultimate” judgements are personal criticism, not representative measurement.

### S14 — “The Murry Brothers Make Ancient Arts a Labor of Love,” *Brøderbund News*, Summer 1988

- Type: publisher profile with developer history and reported interviews; **near-primary / publisher claim**.
- URL: [Newsletter PDF](https://www.mocagh.org/broderbund/broderbundnews-summer88.pdf)
- Used for: the Murrys' mid-1970s programming origins; Dave's electronics work; Barry's air-traffic-control career and FAA training simulation; CP/M Heathkit beginnings; move to IBM graphics; earlier games; selection of tactics as a subject; more than thirty strategy books read; more than eight months of research and programming; and the land-game franchise context.
- Caveat: a promotional company profile written four years later. Biographical specifics and direct recollections are useful, but duration and reading-volume claims were not independently audited.

### S15 — Brøderbund software catalogue entry and designer statement

- Type: later publisher catalogue with direct attributed comments; **primary for product rows and attributed statement / publisher claim for reception**.
- URL: [Catalogue PDF](https://www.mocagh.org/broderbund/broderbund-catalog2.pdf)
- Used for: the designers' statement that real strategy aims at surrender rather than destruction; reported sixteen- or seventeen-hour development days; daily letters and telephone calls from involved players; publisher claims of authentic historical personalities; product listings across Apple, Macintosh, and IBM; and later franchise context.
- Caveat: the catalogue's exact date was not firmly established in this pass, and its purpose is promotional. Platform rows establish later commercial availability, not original launch dates; the volume and loyalty of player contact are unquantified.

## Contemporary reception and platform chronology

### S07 — James Delson, *Family Computing*, June 1985

- Type: contemporary independent family-computing review; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue scan](https://www.adamarchive.org/archive/Media/Magazines/Family%20Computing%20-%20Issue%20%2322%201985%20Jun%20-%20ADAM%20Only.pdf)
- Used for: description as a challenging, multilevel game; age-twelve-and-up recommendation; particular suitability for beginners and experienced wargamers taking a lighter break; 1984 copyright; IBM availability; and Apple/Mac versions still described as planned.
- Caveat: the archive filename foregrounds ADAM material, but the issue contains the relevant review table. The compact entry offers a recommendation, not evidence of long-term retention or a complete system analysis.

### S08 — *Computer Entertainer*, January 1986 awards and conversion notice

- Type: contemporary newsletter/editorial award record; **primary for its own award and report**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/43/Computer_Entertainer_4-10.pdf)
- Used for: *The Ancient Art of War* as the publication's 1985 IBM PC/PCjr Entertainment Program of the Year; distinction from *Kampfgruppe*, which received the all-system strategy/wargame award; and the January 1986 report that an Apple II conversion was still under development.
- Caveat: an editorial selection by one publication, not a formal industry-wide award. “In the works” proves the Apple II port was not treated as an already-established 1984 version by this source, but does not itself fix the eventual ship date.

### S09 — Jerry Pournelle, “Chaos Manor,” *BYTE*, February 1986

- Type: contemporary personal technology column; **secondary / anecdotal reception**.
- URL: [Issue page](https://archive.org/details/byte-magazine-1986-02/page/n301/mode/2up)
- Used for: Pournelle's game-of-the-month selection; his view that it was the best strategic computer wargame he and his sons had encountered; evidence that Sun Tzu could be a formidable opponent; and household rather than specialist-wargame appeal.
- Caveat: explicitly personal and based partly on family experience. It is strong evidence that one prominent reviewer found the game accessible and difficult, not a general difficulty or retention measure.

### S10 — Gareth Powell, “The Best of the Games?”, *Australian Apple Review*, April 1986

- Type: contemporary independent review; **secondary**, with a valuable counterexample on mastery.
- URL: [Article transcription](https://apple2.callapple.org/magazines/aar/bestgames.html)
- Used for: Macintosh availability by April 1986; Apple II non-availability in the reviewer's market and use of the IBM version for colour; the reviewer's immediate attraction; broad accessibility claims; and Paul Beaver's reported abandonment after reaching the point of winning nearly every game.
- Caveat: informal, enthusiastic, and sometimes historically overconfident. The mastery account concerns one software expert and cannot estimate typical abandonment, but it is the clearest direct adverse retention evidence found.

### S11 — Jeff Hurlburt, Macintosh review

- Type: archived period review text; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Review transcription](https://mirrors.apple2.org.za/www.textfiles.com/apple/REVIEWS/ancartofwar.txt)
- Used for: praise for the eight opponents, strategic/tactical transition, animation, campaign editor, documentation, and breadth of audience; description of the Macintosh map as two screens high; friction from squad-by-squad inspection and an incomplete big-picture view; and the reviewer's complaint about the absence of an explicit pause.
- Caveat: the archive does not establish the original publication date and venue with complete certainty. It concerns the Macintosh port, whose mouse control and timing must not be projected onto the 1984 PC version.

### S12 — Armando Lucia, *Macworld*, August 1986

- Type: contemporary independent Macintosh review; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://vintageapple.org/macworld/pdf/MacWorld_8608_August_1986.pdf)
- Used for: Macintosh version 1.0, $44.95 price, 128K/512K support, eleven campaigns, eight personalities, twelve formations, editor, expected many hours of play, accessibility, animation, and slower close battles on a 128K Macintosh.
- Caveat: evaluates a later port. Twelve formations, mouse interaction, performance, and viewport details are not evidence about the original PC release; anticipated hours are reviewer expectation rather than telemetry.

### S13 — Steven Levy, “The Game Hall of Fame,” *Macworld*, December 1986

- Type: contemporary editorial retrospective; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue page](https://archive.org/details/MacWorld_8612_December_1986/page/n119/mode/2up)
- Used for: selection as Macworld's Best Strategy Game with *Balance of Power* as runner-up; praise for clear graphics, tiny animated “movies,” and the construction set; and the playful claim that creative users could derive Hundred Years' War-scale longevity.
- Caveat: one author's qualitative Hall of Fame, not a reader poll or industry award. The longevity language is figurative and not used as observed retention.

### S16 — Catherine D. Miller, “Playing with Fire on Land and Sea,” *PC Magazine*, 12 January 1988

- Type: contemporary independent review of the established franchise; **secondary**.
- URL: [Google Books scan, p. 456](https://books.google.com.au/books?id=3J2ynRxCU78C&pg=PA456)
- Used for: later assessment of the land and sea games as educational and entertaining; confirmation that campaigns, opponents, adjustable rules, and manual history remained prominent in how the title was presented.
- Caveat: the article says the player can command every squad member individually, which conflicts with the original manual's troop-class orders. It is not used for that mechanical claim, and its educational framing is an opinion rather than validated learning evidence.

## Later criticism and aging

### S17 — M. Evan Brooks, “Computer Strategy and Wargames: The Pre-20th Century,” *Computer Gaming World* 75, October 1990

- Type: later capsule review; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_75.pdf)
- Used for: three-of-five “Average–Good” rating; continued judgement that the game was enjoyable and easy to learn; recognition of its editor; criticism that history was largely coincidental and scenarios could be unbalanced.
- Caveat: a brief reassessment six years after the original release and in a changed technical market; it does not identify which platform or every scenario tested.

### S18 — M. Evan Brooks, “An Annotated Listing of Pre-20th Century Wargames,” *Computer Gaming World* 107, June 1993

- Type: updated later capsule review plus stated rating policy; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_107.pdf)
- Used for: two-of-five rating paired with substantially the same praise and criticism as S17; explicit explanation that the author's ratings deteriorate as computer games age and that technical obsolescence can occur rapidly.
- Caveat: the score decline deliberately incorporates historical aging, so it cannot be read as evidence that the underlying design suddenly worsened or that contemporary 1984 players rated it two of five.

- Type: software-preservation object and emulator record; **community infrastructure evidence**.
- URL: [Playable MS-DOS item](https://archive.org/details/msdos_Ancient_Art_of_War_1984)

### S20 — lahmyj, *The Ancient Art of War* FAQ/strategy guide, version 1.04 (2008)

- Type: long-form fan guide begun in 2000; **secondary / community claim**.
- URL: [GameFAQs guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/amiga/277541-the-ancient-art-of-war/faqs/52908)
- Used for: evidence of later detailed strategy study; platform comparisons; report of no official patch and no noticed AI difference among principal versions; Game Generator use; exchanged/downloadable campaigns; copy-protection and compatibility issues; and one player's durable attachment.

### S24 — Apple II box, disk, and manual scan (1989)

- Type: original later-port documentation and photographed package; **primary for the Apple II conversion**.
- URL: [Apple II package scan](https://cvxmelody.net/The%20Ancient%20Art%20of%20War%20-%20Apple%20II%20box%2C%20disk%20%26%20manual%20%281989%20Broderbund%29.pdf)

## Retrospective genre position and canonical status

### S21 — Richard Moss, “Build, gather, brawl, repeat: The history of real-time strategy games,” *Ars Technica* (2017)

- Type: researched retrospective genre history; **secondary**.
- URL: [Article](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2017/09/build-gather-brawl-repeat-the-history-of-real-time-strategy-games/)
- Used for: distinction between later economy/base-building RTS conventions and real-time tactics; earlier precedents including *Legionnaire* and *Stonkers*; placement of *The Ancient Art of War* as an influential real-time-tactics step rather than an uncontested first RTS.
- Caveat: retrospective taxonomy imposed decades after release. Genre boundaries are argumentative, and influence is not the same as documented direct descent.

### S22 — Bruce Shelley, Ensemble Studios blog archive

- Type: archived developer blog recounting another designer's judgement; **secondary / anecdotal industry evidence**.
- URL: [Bruce Shelley archive](https://remember-ensemblestudios.com/ensemble-studios/ensemble-blogs-archive/bruce-shelley-page-3/)
- Used for: record that Ian Fischer, later lead designer on *Age of Empires II* and *Age of Mythology*, argued that *The Ancient Art of War* was the first true RTS.
- Caveat: a reported designer opinion without a detailed taxonomy or demonstrated line of influence. It is retained as a notable counterposition, not treated as settled genre history.

### S23 — GameSpy's 2009 “Greatest PC Games of the '80s” list

- Type: retrospective editorial ranking; **secondary**.
- URLs: [Archived original list](https://web.archive.org/web/20090205073108/http://pc.gamespy.com/articles/950/950214p1.html); [ranking reconstruction](https://www.abandonwaredos.com/game-awards.php?ida=36)
- Used for: placement at number ten in one staff selection of the greatest PC games of the 1980s; evidence of later canonical reputation.
- Caveat: the archived original was not fully machine-readable in this pass, so the placement was corroborated through surviving bibliographic references and the reconstruction. It is a subjective editorial list, not an objective rank or activity measure.

### S27 — Reception bibliography and unverified *White Wolf* review pointer

- Type: tertiary reception bibliography; **known but not fully examined**.
- URL: [Overview and review bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ancient_Art_of_War)
- Underlying work identified: Lisa Stevens, review in *White Wolf* 28 (August/September 1991), commonly reported as a five-of-five assessment with appeal beyond dedicated wargamers.
- Used for: identifying a later review for possible follow-up and recording the boundary of this research pass. It is cited only in the dossier's evidence limitations.
- Caveat: the full correct review page was not verified; the reported score and wording are not used for any central reception claim.

## Known but not fully examined

- An exact 1984 retail ship date for the IBM release was not located. Copyrighted documentation, contemporary IBM-only listings, and museum objects establish the year and primary platform but not the day or month.
- The exact first Macintosh ship date remains uncertain. Contemporary evidence establishes availability by April 1986; some databases and archives assign 1985.
- No complete source code, AI technical paper, or developer account of the opponent algorithms was found. The dossier therefore describes documented behavioural personalities, not speculative implementation architecture.
- The complete verified page of Lisa Stevens's *White Wolf* review was not obtained, and it is excluded from central reception claims.
- The original GameSpy page is archived but was not fully readable through the research interface; the number-ten placement is treated as a corroborated bibliographic fact, not a source of substantive design analysis.
- The longer prose surrounding the compact *Family Computing* entry and every review of every port were not needed once the main mechanical, reception, and chronology questions were saturated.
- Japanese PC-88/PC-98 versions and the Amiga, Atari ST, and Amstrad CPC conversions were not reconstructed mechanically. They establish the breadth and duration of porting, not the original PC interface.
- No audited sales total, systematic review corpus, player telemetry, completion records, balance dataset, or retention study was found.
- Surviving reviews skew favourable, while the fan guide and preservation activity are survivor-biased. Potential replay content is documented much more strongly than typical realised replay.
