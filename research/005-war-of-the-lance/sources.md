# Sources: *War of the Lance*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** SSI's Commodore 64/128 release, published in 1990  
**Lineage evidence:** Apple II original, the DL11 tabletop game *Dragons of Glory*, and SSI's launch-period catalogue  
**Later evidence:** DOS play guides, retrospective criticism, sales history, archives, and the current DOS re-release are used only where their platform and perspective permit.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original manual, packaged object, publisher catalogue or advertisement, original tabletop rules, or another period object.
- **Near-primary:** preservation catalogue or product record grounded in a physical object.
- **Secondary:** independent contemporary review, later critical account, strategy guide, or historical analysis.
- **Tertiary:** community database or review index used for orientation and triangulation.
- **Community testimony:** individual player experience, useful for hypotheses about attraction or friction but not representative measurement.

The thirteen-sheet Australian manual supplied by Barry was rendered page by page and visually inspected. Its image content has a usable machine-readable text layer. Search and extraction were checked against the rendered pages, including the platform instructions, political map, setup, phase order, victory conditions, diplomacy, quest, magic, movement, fatigue, terrain, combat, and appendix material.

The supplied file's SHA-256 digest is **f0316e441f1a119b829e53cb1744e8c9ced75b29f95fa3950d729d9d6a024ebf**. Its PDF metadata labels it a Microsoft DOS manual, but the actual pages explicitly cover Apple II and Commodore 64/128 operation. The MoCAGH landing page identifies the scanned package as an Australian C64 object. Content and object provenance therefore override the misleading metadata.

## Original computer object, documentation, and release identity

### S01 — *War of the Lance* Australian manual and C64 package record

- Type: original manual scan and photographed package; **primary**.
- URLs: [MoCAGH Australian C64 object](https://www.mocagh.org/loadpage.php?getgame=warlanceaus); [manual PDF](https://www.mocagh.org/ssi/warlanceaus-manual.pdf)
- Local research copy: /Users/bwbrook/input/warlanceaus-manual.pdf
- Used for: credits; Apple II and C64/128 platform instructions; one- and two-player modes; mandatory Whitestone role in solo play; campaign and scenario; setup controls; victory conditions; six-year/thirty-turn structure; phase order; country, diplomacy, alignment, alliance, and declaration-of-war systems; champions, quests, discoveries, subversion, reinforcements, and replacements; operation points, fatigue, initiative, movement, terrain, stacking, transport, reconnaissance, combat, dragons, fleets, conquest, and interface; absence of economy, tactical battle orders, scenario construction, or persistent progression.
- Caveat: the manual describes intended rules and contains publisher framing. It does not document the AI's internal algorithm, every probability, exact retail date, or typical play time. The PDF metadata is demonstrably unreliable for platform identity.

### S02 — SSI 1989–90 product catalogue

- Type: period publisher catalogue; **primary for product positioning and listed availability / publisher claim for quality**.
- URL: [Catalogue PDF](https://mocagh.org/ssi/ssi-89-90catalog.pdf)
- Used for: SSI's positioning of *War of the Lance* as its first AD&D computer wargame; Apple and Commodore platform listing; IBM version described as due in winter 1989–90; $39.95 price; campaign premise; and evidence that all platforms should not be collapsed into one 1989 launch.
- Caveat: catalogue schedules can slip and do not prove the first day a product reached shops. Promotional descriptions are evidence of declared positioning, not independent evaluation.

### S03 — SSI advertisement, *Compute!'s Gazette* 79, January 1990

- Type: launch-period publisher advertisement in a C64 magazine; **primary for advertised status**.
- URLs: [Internet Archive issue record](https://archive.org/details/1990-01-computegazette); [direct issue PDF](https://archive.org/download/1990-01-computegazette/Compute_Gazette_Issue_79_1990_Jan.pdf)
- Used for: Apple II availability and C64/IBM “coming soon” wording in January 1990; corroboration that the C64 release belongs to 1990 rather than a simultaneous 1989 multi-platform launch.
- Caveat: an advertisement records the publisher's status when copy was prepared, not an audited shipment date. Magazine lead time prevents day-level dating.

### S04 — Eli's Software Encyclopedia Apple II package record

- Type: catalogued physical software object with package images and metadata; **near-primary**.
- URL: [Apple II object record](https://elisoftware.org/wiki/War_Of_The_Lance_%28Apple_II%2C_5_1/4%22_Disk%29_Strategic_Simulations%2C_Inc._-_1989_USA%2C_Canada_Release)
- Used for: documented 1989 Apple II release; 64K requirement; package copy, product identity, and object-level confirmation of the earliest version.
- Caveat: catalogue dates can inherit box or contributor metadata and do not establish a retail day. It is used with publisher and period-ad evidence rather than alone.

### S05 — MobyGames game, credits, and release records

- Type: community-maintained software database; **tertiary orientation**.
- URL: [Game record](https://www.mobygames.com/game/1481/war-of-the-lance/)
- Used for: triangulation of Apple II 1989 and C64/DOS 1990 chronology; credit comparison; identification of reviews and package material; modern critic and player-rating context.
- Caveat: database rows can conflate dates and platforms. Its small critic and player aggregates are not population measures, and no central rule claim relies on it alone.

## Tabletop lineage

### S06 — Douglas Niles and Tracy Hickman, DL11 *Dragons of Glory*

- Type: original TSR tabletop game rules; **primary for the predecessor design**.
- URLs: [Complete page-turning facsimile](https://anyflip.com/cmjgl/waju/basic); [official product record](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/16917/dl11-dragons-of-glory-1e); [official preview PDF](https://d1vzi28wh99zvq.cloudfront.net/pdf_previews/16917-sample.pdf)
- Used for: whole-continent scale; two coalition roles; neutral countries; hex-and-counter armies; racial terrain, flying, naval, political activation, initiative, winter, replacement, conquest, and draconian systems; six-to-eight-hour campaign estimate; and the explicit ability to use the strategic result as background for an AD&D campaign.
- Caveat: the complete facsimile is hosted by a third-party page-turning service, while the product record and shorter official preview independently confirm product identity and framing. The third-party viewer and official store can present automated-access challenges. The computer game modifies this substrate substantially and does not credit Niles or Hickman as its computer designers.

### S07 — “Advanced *Dragons of Glory* Rules,” *Dragon* 107, March 1986

- Type: period official magazine expansion; **primary for declared tabletop complexity trade-off**.
- URL: [Internet Archive issue PDF](https://archive.org/download/DragonMagazine260_201801/DragonMagazine107.pdf)
- Used for: the statement that the base tabletop game was deliberately designed to move quickly and omitted details for ease of play; evidence that optional winter and added complexity were already part of the design conversation surrounding the predecessor.
- Caveat: this is an expansion to the tabletop game, not a source for computer mechanics. It establishes inherited design context only.

## Contemporary C64 reception

### S08 — *Zzap!64* 61, May 1990

- Type: contemporary independent C64 review; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Original review scan, p. 22](https://www.zzap64.co.uk/cgi-bin/displaypage.pl?issue=061&page=022&thumbstart=0&magazine=zzap)
- Used for: C64 street context and £24.99 price; 80 per cent overall score; atmosphere, interaction, and lastability subscores; praise for depth, detail, intricate strategy, professionalism, and setup options; warning that the product is a true wargame rather than an AD&D role-playing spectacle; and evidence of spring 1990 availability.
- Caveat: one magazine's compact scored review. “Lastability” is an editorial forecast, not observed retention. The review's disk description is not used to resolve packaging variations.

### S09 — Paul Rigby, *The Games Machine* 30, May 1990

- Type: contemporary independent C64 review; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://datassette.s3.us-west-004.backblazeb2.com/revistas/the_games_machine_n.30_mai90.pdf)
- Used for: 84 per cent score; praise for faithfulness, story updates, background, atmosphere, and appeal beyond established wargamers; criticism that quest outcomes offer too little control and feel random; wish for direct tactical control.
- Caveat: a brief individual review. Its recommendations and score are opinions, and its desire for a different tactical game is evidence of expectation mismatch rather than proof of a defective rule.

### S10 — Hartley, Patricia, and Kirk Lesser, “The Role of Computers,” *Dragon* 158, June 1990

- Type: contemporary independent C64 review; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Internet Archive issue PDF](https://archive.org/download/DragonMagazine260_201801/DragonMagazine158.pdf)
- Used for: four-star rating; judgement that fantasy elements increase tactical interest; description as one of the best new tactical wargames; novice difficulty; Highlord advantage in the later scenario; very long play caused by disk access and computer thought; unimpressive graphics; recommendation to skip the animated battle display to save time; and evidence of C64 availability by June.
- Caveat: the recurring review column is enthusiastic and uses “tactical” more broadly than this dossier. Time and difficulty are reviewer observations, not controlled measurements.

### S11 — Ken St. Andre, *QuestBusters* VII/10, October 1990

- Type: contemporary independent review, principally of the PC edition but explicitly aware of C64; **secondary, platform-qualified**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.mocagh.org/questbusters/qbustersVII10.pdf)
- Used for: estimated thirty-to-fifty-hour game and hour-plus turns; roughly eighteen phases; interpretation as three linked games of combat, diplomacy, and quests; praise for visual presentation, depth, complexity, challenge, computer opponent, and head-to-head play; the reviewer's defeat on an easy setting; praise for the optional battle animation; and criticism of limited fine tactical control.
- Caveat: the performance experience is not purely C64-specific and the duration estimate is anecdotal. The reviewer's “easy” label should not be confused with a documented smarter/weaker AI setting; setup changes multiple advantages.

### S12 — *QuestBusters* VII/12, December 1990, strategy tips

- Type: contemporary player/editorial strategy advice; **secondary**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.mocagh.org/questbusters/qbustersVII12.pdf)
- Used for: importance of surrounding enemies, fortifications, racial mountain movement, diplomacy allocation, and the reported AI vulnerability when fleets transport land forces.
- Caveat: tips are practical testimony rather than formal rules documentation. The fleet observation is an anecdotal exploit and may depend on position or port.

### S13 — Brock Fanning, “War of the Lance,” *Compute!* 132, August 1991

- Type: later contemporary C64/128 review; **secondary, platform-specific**.
- URL: [Issue PDF](https://www.commodore.ca/gallery/magazines/compute/Compute-132-02.pdf)
- Used for: assessment as a strong combination of an original wargame system and detailed setting; praise for diplomacy, side quests, magic, excitement, and the clear manual; acknowledgement that play is complicated and slow but worth mastering.
- Caveat: published more than a year after launch and strongly favourable. It provides no retention data and its “perfect combination” language is personal judgement.

## Strategy, player response, and retrospective criticism

### S14 — Stephen Maxson, *War of the Lance* strategy guide, version 1.0 (2001)

- Type: detailed fan guide for the PC version; **secondary / community testimony**.
- URL: [GameFAQs guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/567315-war-of-the-lance/faqs/15178)
- Used for: six-year campaign timing; detailed quest, subversion, diplomatic, movement, combat, and victory strategy; evidence that important mechanics required player reconstruction; reports of recurring AI champion, Neraka-defence, surrounding, and fleet behaviours; and one player's return to the game after eleven years.
- Caveat: written long after release, based on a DOS copy, and without source-code access. It is survivor-biased and cannot prove that every behavioural observation applies identically to C64. Manual contradictions are resolved in favour of the manual.

### S15 — Lemon64 game record, review index, and comments

- Type: C64 preservation database and small community-response page; **tertiary / community testimony**.
- URL: [Lemon64 record](https://www.lemon64.com/game/war-of-the-lance)
- Used for: C64 release year, one-to-two-player and joystick-port information, indexed magazine scores, preservation context, and a player's recollection of roughly forty-five-minute turns and affection despite slowness.
- Caveat: only a handful of votes and comments are present. The average score and completion/ownership fields are far too small and self-selected to estimate audience retention. Primary manual and review scans supersede its rule claims.

### S16 — “Dragonlance, but barely a game,” GameFAQs C64 user review (2003)

- Type: retrospective individual C64 review; **community testimony, adverse case**.
- URL: [Review page](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/c64/916888-war-of-the-lance/reviews/55558)
- Used for: a Dragonlance fan's frustration with lack of hero control, disappointing perceived value of items and griffons, one-unit-at-a-time movement, tedium, and failure of the later good-dragon phase to restore interest.
- Caveat: one person's five-of-ten retrospective account, written thirteen years later. It is useful as a concrete abandonment mechanism, not as representative reception or a fully reliable rules source.

### S17 — Allen Rausch, “A History of D&D Video Games,” GameSpy (2004)

- Type: retrospective genre history; **secondary**.
- URL: [Archived article](https://web.archive.org/web/20040816205211/http://pc.gamespy.com/articles/538/538865p4.html)
- Used for: later classification as a forgotten classic; summary of map warfare, quests, and diplomacy as three interacting components; claim that SSI could be proud of it and that it remained enjoyable.
- Caveat: capsule history rather than a replay study. “Still fun” is the author's critical judgement, and the article does not distinguish every platform.

### S18 — Jimmy Maher, “Opening the Gold Box, Part 5: All That Glitters Is Not Gold,” *The Digital Antiquarian* (2017)

- Type: archival software history reporting publisher sales records; **secondary**.
- URL: [Article](https://www.filfre.net/2017/03/opening-the-gold-box-part-5-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold/)
- Used for: reported sales of 15,255 copies and comparison with much larger SSI AD&D role-playing releases; evidence that favourable reviews did not translate into equivalent mass commercial reach.
- Caveat: the underlying SSI sales ledger was not available for independent inspection in this pass. The exact number is reported through the historian, and sales do not directly measure satisfaction or hours played.

### S19 — RetroArchives, “War of the Lance”

- Type: modern multi-platform retrospective; **secondary, explicitly later**.
- URL: [Review](https://retroarchives.fr/war-of-the-lance/)
- Used for: platform comparison; C64 colour, readability, joystick, silence, and slow AI; praise for ambition, asymmetry, and diplomacy; criticism of passivity, opaque information, quest randomness, fixed-deadline effects on remote allies, initiative double turns, and long waits for agency.
- Caveat: a modern French retrospective with hindsight and emulator-era expectations. Its 10.5/20 C64 score is not contemporary evidence, and platform details were checked against the manual where possible.

### S20 — Super Adventures in Gaming, *War of the Lance* section (2026)

- Type: modern DOS play diary by a self-described non-wargamer; **secondary / novice-experience testimony**.
- URL: [Article](https://superadventuresingaming.blogspot.com/2026/02/dungeons-dragons-games-vol-10-silver.html)
- Used for: friction from indistinct borders, generic stack icons, extra movement clicks, path following, uncertain alliances, perceived scenario hopelessness, early Whitestone powerlessness, silent/basic presentation, and abandonment after roughly five turns.
- Caveat: DOS rather than C64, deliberately non-expert, and based on a short encounter rather than mastery. It is especially useful for onboarding failure, not for judging strategic depth or the C64's exact performance.

## Evidential balance

The central reconstruction rests primarily on the C64/Apple manual and period C64 reviews. The board-game rules establish lineage but not identity. DOS guides and modern play diaries supply hypotheses about fixed AI behaviour, mastery, and onboarding only where they agree with common rules or are explicitly platform-qualified.

The reception evidence contains both strong period praise and concrete adverse cases. None of the following is treated as representative telemetry:

- magazine percentages or star ratings;
- a fan's eleven-year attachment;
- a critic's five-turn abandonment;
- a small C64 comment page;
- collection-level Steam or GOG reviews.

Together they show plausible mechanisms of attraction, retention, and loss. They do not reveal how Barry experienced the game; that evidence remains reserved for the later corpus-wide discussion.
