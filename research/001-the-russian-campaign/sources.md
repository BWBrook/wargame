# Sources: *The Russian Campaign*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Scope:** The 1974–1978 design lineage, with later editions and organised play used as retrospective evidence.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** rules, direct designer/developer statement, publisher history, or official results.
- **Near-primary:** contemporary in-house design analysis or company retrospective.
- **Secondary:** independent review, retrospective, or community record.
- **Publisher claim:** useful but potentially promotional; not independently verified.

## Core rules and publication sources

### S01 — Compass Games, “The Russian Campaign, Original 1974 Edition”

- Type: publisher page; **publisher claim**.
- URL: [Compass Games product page](https://www.compassgames.com/product/the-russian-campaign-original-1974-edition/)
- Used for: the restoration's intended relationship to the 1974 Jedko game; designer credit; scale, players, advertised complexity and duration; component list.
- Caveat: promotional language and duration claims.

### S02 — Compass Games, *The Russian Campaign: Original 1974 Edition* rules (2021)

- Type: restored original-lineage rules; **primary for the 2021 restoration, near-primary for 1974 mechanics**.
- URL: [Rules PDF](https://compassgamesbucket.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/downloads/TRC_FINAL_RULES_v4_11.12.2020+%281%29.pdf)
- Used for: sequence, movement, double impulses, ZOCs, combat, retreats, automatic victory, weather, supply, rail/sea movement, replacements, workers, Stukas, partisans, leaders, and basic victory conditions.
- Caveat: the publisher describes it as the original design, but the presentation is modern and a 2022 clarification sheet corrected several points.

### S03 — Consim Press/GMT, *The Russian Campaign: Deluxe Fifth Edition* rules (2022/2023)

- Type: later rules plus developer notes; **primary for fifth edition; retrospective evidence for lineage**.
- URL: [Deluxe Fifth Edition rules PDF](https://gamers-hq.de/media/pdf/a5/f0/40/Russian-Campaign_FINAL_web.pdf)
- Used for: detailed examples of tactical interactions; modern scenario structure; optional rules; developer account of retained core principles, accumulated tournament knowledge, revised weather, rail complexity, replacements, and variants.
- Caveat: substantially later than Barry's likely copy. The developer's “almost 250 thousand copies” statement is not independently verified.

### S04 — “Interview: John Edwards,” *The General* 15(1), 1978, pp. 16–17

- Type: direct designer interview; **primary**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2015%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: design genealogy from *Stalingrad*; repeated solitaire play; reading and design process; preference for short rules, rapid engagement, player involvement, fun, and restrained detail; comments on Jedko versus Avalon Hill editions.
- Caveat: in-house Avalon Hill interview; some disagreement with reviewer Richard DeBaun is documented in the following issue.

### S05 — Richard Hamblen, “TRC The Second Time Around,” *The General* 14(1), 1977, pp. 13–17

- Type: contemporary developer analysis; **near-primary**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2014%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: changes in the revised rules; original draw problem; rationale and operation of secret Sudden Death objectives; scenario revision; weather-roll concerns and optional alternative; tricky-tactics clarifications.
- Caveat: Avalon Hill's account of its own development work.

### S06 — “An Interview with Richard Hamblen,” *The General* 16(5), 1980, p. 42

- Type: developer/designer interview; **primary opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2016%20No%205.pdf)
- Used for: praise of alternative strategies, tactical variety, offensive and defensive agency for both sides, historical feel, and the tradeoff between playability and realism.

### S07 — Charles S. Roberts Awards history and 2023 results

- Type: official award record; **primary**.
- URLs: [Award history](https://charlessrobertsawards.com/history/); [2023 nominees and winners](https://charlessrobertsawards.com/2023-charles-s-roberts-awards-winners-announced/)
- Used for: 1976 Best Strategic Game award and the Deluxe Fifth Edition's 2023 operational-game nomination.

### S08 — Avalon Hill Company Index, 1952–1980

- Type: transcription of Avalon Hill's company history; **near-primary**.
- URL: [BoardGameGeek wiki transcription](https://boardgamegeek.com/wiki/page/Avalon_Hill_Company_Index%2C_1952-1980)
- Used for: 1976 publication, 1977 and 1978 revisions, development credits, competition reception, displacement of *Stalingrad*, and the statement that sales were not outstanding.
- Caveat: hosted transcription rather than the original printed index.

## Reception, criticism, and longevity

### S09 — David Garvin, “The Russian Campaign: The Classic that Refuses to Die” (2023)

- Type: enthusiast retrospective; **secondary**.
- URL: [No Dice No Glory article](https://nodicenoglory.com/the-russian-campaign-the-classic-that-refuses-to-die/)
- Used for: six-page-rulebook comparison; simplicity versus tactical implication; contact traps; extensive opening literature; chess analogy; replayability and ahistorical outcomes.

### S10 — JCB III, “TAHGC, The Russian Campaign (1976)” (2009)

- Type: detailed retrospective profile; **secondary**.
- URL: [Map and Counters article](https://mapandcounters.blogspot.com/2009/08/tahgc-russian-campaign-1977.html)
- Used for: component and duration estimates; easy and logical core; sweeping action; Stuka criticism; supply/ZOC summary; continued ownership and replay appeal.
- Caveat: personal assessment; map scale is explicitly estimated and differs from Compass's figure.

### S11 — “My Forty Years on the Eastern Front: A Boardgaming Life Game Survey” (2015)

- Type: extended personal retrospective; **secondary and deliberately critical**.
- URL: [The Boardgaming Life article](https://theboardgaminglife.com/2015/01/18/strife-in-the-living-room-my-forty-years-on-the-eastern-front-a-boardgaming-life-game-survey/)
- Used for: contrast with *Stalingrad*; praise for double movement and threatened encirclement; criticism of later scenarios, absent Soviet air, coarse modelling, and amendment dependence.
- Caveat: strongly voiced individual perspective.

### S12 — Mark Dultz, “Long Trek Eastwards,” *The General* 22(4), 1985

- Type: hobby-magazine analytical article; **secondary contemporary-to-lineage analysis**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2022%20No%204.pdf)
- Used for: the magnitude of September/October and November/December weather swings and the rationale for revised weather resolution.

### S13 — “Momentum in the Valley” (2014)

- Type: historical-game design critique; **secondary**.
- URL: [Wargame_[space] essay](https://www.wargamespace.com/2014/10/01/momentum-in-the-valley/)
- Used for: critique of perfect-information hindsight, continuous encirclement, lost operational pauses, and overreliance on weather to explain the end of German momentum.

### S14 — Boardgame Players Association / World Boardgaming Championships records

- Type: official tournament records; **primary for organised-play activity**.
- URLs: [Event history through 2013](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbkex/trcpge.htm); [2024 event report](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbook24/trc.html)
- Used for: continuous event history from 1991, participation levels, play-by-email history, and the 2024 report of 22 players and 48 games.

### S15 — Art Kritzer, “A Fond Friendship Renewed,” *The General* 28(3), 1992, pp. 46–49

- Type: retrospective from a long-time player; **secondary**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2028%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: repeated-play appeal, return after years away, suitability of the six-page rules for an evening, and continued evocative pull of the pieces and systems.

### S16 — Tom Chick, “War in the East: I shiver in my bones just thinking about the weather” (2011)

- Type: computer-wargame essay with comparison to TRC; **secondary**.
- URL: [Quarter to Three article](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2011/05/05/war-in-the-east-i-shiver-in-my-bones-just-thinking-about-the-weather/)
- Used for: concise retrospective statement that late-1941 weather could effectively decide the campaign and that historical-weather variants were used for balance.

### S17 — Dean Miller, “Initial Impressions of The Russian Campaign,” *The General* 13(3), 1976, pp. 7–10

- Type: contemporary analysis; **secondary, published by Avalon Hill**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2013%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: early praise of the game alongside criticism of historical abstraction and Avalon Hill's Automatic Victory restrictions; strategic analysis of attrition, worker cities, role reversal, and the Axis defensive transition.

### S18 — Grognard.com TRC archive

- Type: community archive; **secondary**.
- URLs: [TRC archive index](https://grognard.com/ArticlePageList.aspx?GameID=r945); [short review](https://grognard.com/reviews/russcam.txt); [rules Q&A](https://grognard.com/errata/trc.txt); [Russian Campaign II contents](https://grognard.com/info/ruscam2.txt)
- Used for: simple sequence; both sides' aggressive phases; weather centrality; postal play suitability; evidence of rail/ZOC clarifications; 1986 update contents.
- Caveat: archived individual postings, dates and provenance incomplete.

### S19 — BoardGameGeek, *The Russian Campaign: Deluxe 5th Edition*

- Type: community database and user comments; **secondary/current snapshot**.
- URL: [Game page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/208974/the-russian-campaign-deluxe-5th-edition)
- Used for: modern listed duration/complexity, comments on accessibility, optional-rule layering, and examples of first-campaign duration.
- Caveat: self-selected ratings and comments; applies to the 2023 edition, not the 1970s physical game.

### S20 — “The Russian Campaign” overview

- Type: tertiary summary; **orientation only**.
- URL: [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Russian_Campaign)
- Used for: cross-checking publication lineage, physical variations of the 1974 printing, early reception references, and later descendants.
- Caveat: not used as sole support for central claims where primary or stronger secondary evidence was available.
