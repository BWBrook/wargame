# Sources: *V for Victory: Gold-Juno-Sword*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** Atomic Games/Three-Sixty Pacific's DOS and Macintosh *V for Victory: Gold-Juno-Sword* (1993)  
**Series evidence:** The first three *V for Victory* battle sets, the 1995 *Commemorative Collection*, and Atomic's later Avalon Hill work are used only where labelled for lineage, shared rules, reception expectations, or afterlife.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original manual or software documentation, direct developer statement, preserved period advertisement, or period magazine page containing direct designer material.
- **Near-primary:** contemporary player discussion, institutional object record, or period report closely tied to the release.
- **Secondary:** independent contemporary review, later criticism, retrospective interview, or historical analysis.
- **Tertiary:** database, catalogue, index, or bibliographic orientation; not sole support for a central mechanical claim.
- **Publisher/community claim:** evidence of intention, custodianship, practice, or remembered engagement, but potentially promotional, anecdotal, selected, or survivor-biased.

The original Operations Manual and 1995 Commemorative Reference Manual were downloaded as PDFs. The Operations Manual was rendered page by page and visually inspected in full; OCR was used only for navigation because headings, tables, and decorated text produce errors. Key pages of the collection reference were checked against its text layer. The collection manual has editorial residue from earlier battle sets, so the original Operations Manual controls *Gold-Juno-Sword*-specific claims.

Relevant pages from *Computer Games Strategy Plus*, *Computer Gaming World*, *Electronic Entertainment*, *High Score Express*, and *The General* were rendered and visually inspected. OCR or binary-string extraction was used only to locate material. Where a period page was found only through a catalogue or transcription, that limitation is stated explicitly.

## Manuals and original mechanical evidence

### S01 — *V for Victory: Gold-Juno-Sword Operations Manual*

- Type: original release manual; **primary**.
- URLs: [MyAbandonware object and manual page](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/v-for-victory-gold-juno-sword-1mm); [direct manual PDF](https://d3.xp.myabandonware.com/f/ly6y/V-for-Victory-Gold-Juno-Sword_Manual_DOS_EN.pdf)
- Used for: title styling; Atomic Games and Three-Sixty Pacific identity; complete design, programming, production, art, research, and manual credits; version 4.0 status; historical situation; D+1 start and designer rationale for omitting the landings; one-kilometre hex and four-hour turn scale; Commonwealth and German forces; scenario framing; The Orne Bridges tutorial; campaign duration; scenario variants; random hidden variants; weather; air-balance options; optional realism; victory objectives and values; British replacement and naval-use deductions; six victory levels; historical Marginal German Victory; staff-assistant commands; and interface framing around selectable involvement.
- Important discrepancy: the introduction says that seven scenarios are available, then refers to six basic scenarios, while another section says six scenarios are listed. This remains unresolved. Game Bytes also reports seven; see S07.
- Editorial caveat: a weather passage refers to Holland although the game is set in Normandy. It is treated as a copy error. The manual's rules and tables were checked visually rather than accepted from OCR.
- Hosting caveat: MyAbandonware is an unofficial preservation site. It is used as a host for an original manual scan and as evidence of current preservation practice, not as authority for copyright or present commercial availability.

### S02 — *V for Victory: Commemorative Collection Reference Manual* (1995)

- Type: official collection-era common-engine manual; **primary for shared documented rules / near-primary for the exact 1993 4.0 state**.
- URL: [Reference Manual PDF](https://freebie.games/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/V_for_Victory_-_Commemorative_Collection_-_Reference_Manual_-_PC.pdf)
- Used for: planning, simultaneous execution, and after-action phases; unit scale and headquarters abstraction; tactical, strategic, passenger, automatic, stack, and divisional movement; route plotting; bridges and engineers; stacking; zones of control; attack and defence postures; artillery, air, and naval mission types; combat factors; regimental integrity; supply priorities, trace, stockpiles, progressive out-of-supply state, and headquarters relationships; morale, fatigue, disruption, replacement, fortification, and weather systems; Limited Intelligence, friendly Fog of War, and One Division Per Attack; overlays and map views; staff-assistant workflow; hot-seat information handling; saving, autosave, and route reconstruction.
- Important boundary: this is a 1995 four-game consolidation, not the untouched Reference Manual shipped with *Gold-Juno-Sword*. Some wording retains *Utah Beach* assumptions. A “What is Missing” section is a retrospective critique of an earlier baseline and cannot be assigned wholesale to version 4.0.
- Caveat: the hosted PDF's provenance is weaker than an institutional scan, but the document is internally consistent with the original Operations Manual and period review descriptions. Claims unique to a battle set are not taken from it.

## Direct designer and developer evidence

### S03 — Eric Young and Keith Zabalaoui, *Gold-Juno-Sword* design preview, *Computer Games Strategy Plus* 30, May 1993

- Type: contemporary article containing direct designer material; **primary for design intent / near-primary for pre-release features**.
- URL: [Internet Archive issue record](https://archive.org/details/computer-games-strategy-plus-30-may-1993)
- Used for: D+1-to-2-July campaign frame; designer rationale for beginning after the landings; description of the battle as an attritional “slug-fest”; Allied abundance versus the German need to conserve and counterattack selectively; version 4.0 changes to logistics, naval command, limited intelligence, headquarters, artillery, and artificial-opponent decisiveness; improved supply handling; and positioning of variants as plausible alternatives rather than arbitrary enemy-strength bonuses.
- Caveat: this is a preview before release and partly a designer's own account. Claimed improvements are development intentions, not independent validation. Final rules and credits are checked against S01–S02.

### S10 — Keith Zabalaoui interview, *The General* 29(1), Avalon Hill

- Type: direct developer interview during Atomic's transition to Avalon Hill; **primary for studio recollection and production practice / secondary for lineage**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2029%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: Atomic as an independent design group; *V for Victory* as its best-known prior work; the Avalon Hill relationship; continuity of tried-and-tested systems into *Operation Crusader*; comparison of *V for Victory*'s one-kilometre, battalion-level scale with the later game's changed scale; design on Macintosh; a single portable Macintosh/IBM source base; and Atomic's stated preference for challenging opponents rather than superhuman forces or overt cheats.
- Caveat: the interview concerns Atomic's wider practice and forthcoming Avalon Hill work. Its AI philosophy is not a performance test of *Gold-Juno-Sword*, and later engine claims are not projected backwards as release features.

## Contemporary independent reviews and awards

### S04 — Terry Lee Coleman, “It Was the Best of Times, It Was the Worst of Times,” *Computer Gaming World* 113, December 1993

- Type: detailed independent contemporary review; **secondary**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_113.pdf)
- Used for: praise of SVGA graphics, menus, Quick Start material, manuals, physical aids, victory information, exhaustive rules, and general interface accomplishment; criticism of omitting the assault landings, the weak German strategic position, long campaign, no modem play, save/version issues, missing visible combat-results framework, and artificial-opponent failures in artillery coordination, assaults, exploitation, regrouping, and replacements; distinction between an Allied AI made threatening by material superiority and competent decision-making; and the reviewer's judgement that technical accomplishment did not create an emotionally satisfying campaign.
- Caveat: one unusually severe review. Its account is highly specific and therefore valuable adverse evidence, but its claims about feeling, balance, and “soul” are critical judgement rather than universal player response. Only brief phrases are quoted; the dossier otherwise paraphrases.

### S05 — *V for Victory: Gold-Juno-Sword* review, *Electronic Entertainment* 1(1), January 1994

- Type: independent contemporary review; **secondary**.
- URLs: [Internet Archive issue record](https://archive.org/details/ElectronicEntertainment01Jan1994); [RetroMags issue catalogue](https://www.retromags.com/magazines/usa/electronic-entertainment/electronic-entertainment-vol1-no1/)
- Used for: 8/10 rating; comparison with a detailed board wargame without the burden of manipulating a very large counter set; point-and-click operation; the option to let a staff assistant handle minutiae; individual and stack orders; computer or human opposition; and interpretation of the scenarios as a progressive learning sequence.
- Caveat: a short favourable review rather than a technical audit. It does not test long-campaign AI or measure learning time and retention.

### S06 — *Gold-Juno-Sword* review, *High Score Express* 21, 1993

- Type: independent contemporary Swedish review; **secondary**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://dmzarkivet.se/pdf/HighScore-199321.pdf)
- Used for: 4/5 rating; PC test with Macintosh availability noted; praise for graphics and detail; view that the computer assists exactly as much as the player wants; particular approval of computer-administered supply; assessment that an enormous system remains manageable and clear; and support for either-side, computer, and two-player play with variants.
- Language caveat: the relevant Swedish page was rendered and visually inspected. The dossier uses a conservative English paraphrase, not an asserted official translation.
- Caveat: one positive period review. It establishes perceived accessibility for that reviewer, not typical mastery or campaign completion.

### S07 — Chris Merchant, *Gold-Juno-Sword* review, Game Bytes 15

- Type: independent contemporary electronic-magazine review; **secondary, recovered from an obsolete binary distribution**.
- URLs: [DOSGames Game Bytes index](https://www.dosgames.com/gamebytes.php); [ibiblio Game Bytes archive](https://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/gbindex.html)
- Used for: report of seven scenarios; estimate of roughly sixty hours for the longest campaign; the reviewer's claim to have learned basic interface operation within about five minutes without first reading the manual; generally adequate and occasionally surprising AI combined with conspicuously foolish moves; absence of PBEM and editor; and recommendation to serious wargamers.
- Recovery caveat: issue 15 survives in an old packaged data format. Clear text was recovered through binary-string inspection; layout and some punctuation are damaged. Only unambiguous claims are used, and no precise wording is quoted.
- Caveat: play-time and learning-time statements are one reviewer's estimates, not instrumented measurements.

### S08 — *Computer Gaming World* Premier Awards, issue 119, June 1994

- Type: contemporary editorial award page; **primary for finalist status / secondary for evaluation**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_119.pdf)
- Used for: *Gold-Juno-Sword* as a Wargame of the Year finalist; *Clash of Steel* as the winner; and the panel's recognition of improved artillery, supply, and cleaner interface in the sequel/battle-set line.
- Caveat: award-panel judgement is not a review consensus or retention measure. It is particularly useful because it preserves positive editorial recognition after S04's severe review in the same magazine.

- Type: contemporary Usenet player discussion; **near-primary for player practice and version reports / community claim for quality**.
- URL: [Google Groups thread](https://groups.google.com/g/comp.sys.mac.games/c/-knijhSTE6g)

## Evidence excluded or kept peripheral

- General web summaries and copied abandonware descriptions were used only to find stronger sources.
- Wikipedia was not used as authority for mechanics, credits, AI, reviews, or release boundaries.
- Search-result snippets were not treated as evidence where the underlying page could not be inspected.
- Current abandonware downloads were not executed. No claim about gameplay, patch state, malware safety, or legal status is made from their presence.
- The later *World at War* games were not mechanically reconstructed.
- The Commemorative Reference Manual's “What is Missing” list was not assigned to *Gold-Juno-Sword* because its baseline is an earlier battle-set state.
- No inferred scenario list was assembled from screenshots, filenames, or uncertain OCR.
- No player quote was treated as a representative view, and no current rating was treated as a retention rate.

## Main uncertainties carried into the dossier

1. Whether the shipped scenario count should be read as six, seven, or six basic scenarios plus one differently classified scenario.
2. Which exact earlier battle-set and save-data combinations work under version 4.0 without upgrade or patching.
3. Whether DOS and Macintosh differed materially in performance or interface beyond platform presentation.
4. How the opponent and staff assistant were implemented internally, and how much code they shared.
5. How often automatic pathing or save reconstruction produced operationally significant route changes.
6. Whether a competent Allied AI impression chiefly reflected its material advantage.
7. Typical scenario completion, campaign completion, replay, and return rates.
8. The exact reach of any current licensed specialist distribution outside the major stores checked.
