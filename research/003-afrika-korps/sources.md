# Sources: *Afrika Korps*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** Avalon Hill's 1964 first edition  
**Later evidence:** The 1965 revision, 1978 repackage, 1980 rules rewrite, strategy literature, variants, reviews, and organised play are used to reconstruct the common system and diagnose durability, ambiguity, luck, and balance—not silently substituted for the first release.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original-period rules, ruling, direct designer/developer statement, publisher record, or official event result.
- **Near-primary:** in-house historical account, faithful hosted reconstruction, or contemporary house-magazine evidence close to production.
- **Secondary:** independent review, strategy article, retrospective, or community record.
- **Tertiary:** orientation source aggregating other accounts; not sole support for central claims where stronger evidence is available.
- **Publisher/community claim:** useful evidence of perception or practice, but potentially promotional or survivor-biased.

## Contemporary launch, rulings, and repair

### S04 — *The General* 1(1), May 1964

- Type: launch-period publisher magazine; **primary for contemporary publisher statements**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2001%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: launch timing; *Afrika Korps* as the first magazine contest; reported shift toward a German advantage with experienced play; recommendation to reduce the German supply maximum from three wagons to two; explicit rationale that scarcity prevents attacking every turn and forces battle selection; early sea-movement ruling later reversed.
- Caveat: in-house commentary and initial experience, not controlled balance evidence. The issue's importance is chronological: it discusses capacity rather than a random supply-arrival table.

### S05 — *The General* 1(2), July 1964

- Type: contemporary publisher supplement, letters, and player proposal; **primary for rulings, mixed opinion for balance**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2001%20No%202.pdf)
- Used for: reversal of the Benghazi sea-movement ruling; statement that Germany lacks first-turn supply and cannot attack; introduction of the seasonal Supply Chart as a suggested realism/balance mechanism; Victor Madeja's report of repeated British collapse and proposed setup/replacement changes; evidence of immediate public repair.
- Caveat: combines official rulings with individual player advocacy. The exact early first-turn convention later conflicted with the 1965 postal procedure.

### S06 — *The General* 1(3), September 1964

- Type: contemporary house-magazine player response and variant; **primary for publication, secondary opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2001%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: Jon Perica's “Absurdity of Afrika Korps,” reporting club rejection of the no-first-turn-supply and random-supply changes as an Allied overcorrection; evidence that isolation and sea-movement changes were separately evaluated; continuing historical/map variant work.
- Caveat: forceful advocacy from one club, not a representative survey or statistical balance test.

### S07 — *The General* 1(6), March 1965

- Type: publisher magazine rules aid; **primary for the 1965 revision state**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2001%20No%206.pdf)
- Used for: explicit statement that the latest instruction revision incorporated a German Supply Table requiring an arrival determination; postal-play implementation; contradictory provision of an automatic initial German supply; evidence that the chart had moved from July suggestion into the revised rule tradition.
- Caveat: the postal “stock” procedure is a proxy for a die roll and should not be used as a clean transcription of face-to-face rules. Its first-turn provision conflicts with S05 and later convention.

### S08 — *The General* 4(3), 1967

- Type: publisher magazine notice; **primary for product maintenance**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2004%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: replacement-parts notice identifying the latest instruction folder with die-roll Supply Table and the earlier Battle Manual copyright as 1964; evidence that revised material continued to circulate alongside the original game.
- Caveat: administrative evidence, not a full edition comparison.

## Mature rules and development account

### S09 — Open third-edition rules reconstruction

- Type: open, modern typeset reconstruction of the Avalon Hill third-edition system; **secondary rules reconstruction**.
- URLs: [Project](https://gitlab.com/wargames_tex/afrikakorps_tex); [rules source](https://gitlab.com/wargames_tex/afrikakorps_tex/-/raw/master/rules.tex)
- Used for: 38 half-month turns; map and unit conventions; turn sequence; terrain, coastal road, stacking and zones; supply arrival, capture, movement, support radius, and expenditure; reinforcements and replacements; Rommel; mandatory combat, soak-offs, combat odds, retreats, Automatic Victory, isolation, and victory conditions.
- Caveat: a fan-made reconstruction maintained decades after publication, not an image of Avalon Hill's 1964 or 1980 rulebook. It regularises and illustrates the mature rules and is never sole evidence that a procedure appeared unchanged in 1964.

### S10 — Jonathan Lockwood, “Afrika Korps Theory,” *The General* 17(3), 1980

- Type: expert strategy article plus publisher announcement of revised rules; **secondary analysis / near-primary development context**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2017%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: publisher claims of front-line sales and extensive competition; announcement that the rules had been completely rewritten without fundamental change to play; stereotyped tournament openings; force, space, time, and luck framework; attritional novice versus positional expert; supply-dependent Axis and time-dependent Allied strategy; developed gambits and counter-openings.
- Caveat: advanced tournament perspective and publisher promotion. Named openings assume mature rulings, not the 1964 first-print environment.

### S11 — Frank Preissle, “Face-Lift for a Classic Beauty,” *The General* 18(1), 1981

- Type: direct account by the 1980 rewrite's developer/editor; **primary for revision intent, secondary opinion on play**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2018%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: why rewriting was necessary; ambiguous and uncovered situations; contradictory official answers and absence of R&D staff during 1964–71; Old Guard tribal knowledge; cosmetic clarification intent; easy-to-learn/years-to-master assessment; close-balance and mistake-severity claims; luck as Achilles heel; specific clarifications to supply capture, Automatic Victory, isolation, sea movement, Exchanges, and Rommel; proposed deterministic supply and anti-gamble Tobruk variants.
- Caveat: an insider defending the rewrite and praising a publisher classic. His “no change” framing coexists with individual clarified procedures that can alter outcomes.

### S12 — *The General* cumulative index, volumes 1–20

- Type: magazine bibliographic index; **primary for publication history**.
- URL: [Index PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/z02%20The%20General%20Index%20Vol%201%20to%2020.pdf)
- Used for: breadth and persistence of *Afrika Korps* material—basic and advanced strategy, history, variants, contests, replays, openings, tournament play, play-by-mail, theory, puzzles, and repeated questions and answers.
- Caveat: an index proves that material appeared, not that each article's claims were correct.

## Strategy, mastery, luck, and session form

### S13 — Omar L. DeWitt, “A Decade with Das Afrika Korps,” *The General* 11(5), 1975

- Type: long-time player's strategy retrospective; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2011%20No%205.pdf)
- Used for: eleven years of continued personal enjoyment; differences from the original version; broad-manœuvre opening and chess-like El Alamein endgame; supply scarcity; isolation and Automatic Victory analysis; claim that fewer battles increase luck; argument that indirect manoeuvre can minimise it; acknowledgement that premature low-odds Tobruk attacks turn the game into a dice game.
- Caveat: one successful expert's strongly favourable account, including the candid admission that winning most of his games contributed to enjoyment.

### S14 — Dave Roberts, “Competitional Afrika Korps,” *The General* 14(1), 1977

- Type: expert critique and redesign variant; **secondary analytical opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2014%20No%201.pdf)
- Used for: months-long intensive study culture; reported expert view that too much can ride on one die; novice's lucky 1:1 at Tobruk and resulting expert refusal; praise for the otherwise charmingly simple game and imaginative manoeuvre; counterpoint that some luck complaints conceal poor play; specific variant goals to retain simple clarity while reducing luck and increasing historical texture.
- Caveat: both criticism and proposed fixes come from one competitive tradition; the variant is diagnostic evidence, not part of the original game.

### S15 — Tom Hazlett, “Tournament Play,” *The General* 11(3), 1974

- Type: tournament-format analysis; **secondary / organiser-player opinion**.
- URL: [Magazine PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2011%20No%203.pdf)
- Used for: estimate that competent full play can require six to seven hours; defensive incentive to use most of the 38 turns; postal duration; argument that shorter tournament clocks encourage aggressive, risky play and change the game's natural incentives.
- Caveat: duration and behaviour depend on player skill, pace, and event rules; not a universal playtime test.

## Published reception and personal retrospectives

### S16 — Nicholas Palmer, *The Comprehensive Guide to Board Wargaming* (1977), p. 128

- Type: contemporary independent guide; **secondary review**.
- URL: [Book scan PDF](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/Comprehensive%20Guide%20to%20Board%20Wargaming.pdf)
- Used for: direct assessment of *Afrika Korps* as an operational classic; continually changing front, mobility and flank probes; well-balanced, fast-moving, easy-to-learn, but rather luck-dependent; two-to-four-hour estimate.
- Caveat: compact capsule review rather than extended analysis; evaluates the revised classic available in 1977, not necessarily the literal 1964 rules.

### S17 — General overview with contemporary reception bibliography

- Type: **tertiary orientation**, with bibliographic pointers to published reviews.
- URL: [Overview and reception references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Korps_%28game%29)
- Underlying works identified:
  - Mike Nethercot, *Games & Puzzles* 7 (November 1972), p. 21.
  - Nicholas Palmer, *The Comprehensive Guide to Board Wargaming* (1977), p. 128; inspected directly as S16.

### S18 — Warwell, “Retrospective: Afrika Korps” (2014)

- Type: personal retrospective; **secondary anecdotal evidence**.
- URL: [Retrospective](https://warwellwg.blogspot.com/2014/12/retrospective-afrika-korps.html)
- Used for: learning the game at roughly age ten; simple rules as entry into adult wargaming; repeated play and repeated German failure at Tobruk; remembered intellectual and imaginative appeal.
- Caveat: one person's favourable memory decades later, vulnerable to nostalgia and not representative of general retention.

### S19 — Map and Counters, “AH Afrika Korps 1964–1980” (2009)

- Type: enthusiast historical overview; **secondary**.
- URL: [Overview](https://mapandcounters.blogspot.com/2009/04/ah-afrika-korps-1964-1980.html)
- Used for: component and campaign overview; design innovations; map, supply, Automatic Victory, road, Rommel, isolation, and Tobruk roles; low rules volume versus high mastery; long-lived expert field.
- Caveat: advocate's synthesis of multiple editions and accumulated play convention.

### S20 — Map and Counters, “Afrika Korps Notebook: AV Supply Swap” (2010)

- Type: expert tactical essay; **secondary analytical evidence**.
- URL: [Supply-swap analysis](https://mapandcounters.blogspot.com/2010/05/afrika-korps-notebook-av-supply-swap.html)
- Used for: exact interaction among physical supply, capture, immediate reuse, and Automatic Victory; claim that those two innovations define much of the game's distinctive appeal; anecdote of a decades-experienced opponent encountering a novel supply-chain tactic.
- Caveat: a specialised mature-rules interaction and one anecdote; not proof that typical players discover or enjoy it.

### S21 — Map and Counters, “Game Analysis: Afrika Korps” (2009)

- Type: long-time player's Tobruk analysis; **secondary opinion and counterargument**.
- URL: [Tobruk analysis](https://mapandcounters.blogspot.com/2009/09/game-analysis-afrika-korps.html)
- Used for: recurring criticism that a low-odds Tobruk roll can decide the campaign and cause players to stop; counterargument that such attacks are often chosen under tournament pressure and that untimed positional manoeuvre remains the game's strength; evidence of forty-year attachment.
- Caveat: the author is an explicit defender of the game. The value is the clear statement of both the criticism and an informed rebuttal, not a neutral verdict.

## Known but not fully examined

- A complete, photographed 1964 rulebook was not located. This is the principal evidential gap and the reason every back-projected rule is qualified.
- The complete original Nethercot, Freeman, Campion, and McArthur review pages identified through S17 were not inspected. Their views are represented cautiously and never provide sole support for a central claim.
- Not every *General* item listed in S12 was read. S04–S07 and S10–S15 deliberately sample launch repair, mature rules development, praise, criticism, strategy, and tournament form.
- The long fan “Afrika Korps Thesis” and every historical redesign were not needed once the core evidence was saturated; they remain possible follow-up sources if a later comparative audit needs a narrower question.
- No systematic review corpus, sales audit, retention dataset, or complete balance database was found.
- Current organised-play evidence is precise but survivor-biased: it describes a committed specialist community, not the median 1964 buyer.
