# Sources: *Panzer General*, *Fantasy General*, and *Panzer Corps*

**Research date:** 2026-08-23  
**Primary scope:** SSI's DOS *Panzer General* (1994), SSI's DOS *Fantasy General* (1996), and the original Windows *Panzer Corps: Wehrmacht* (2011)  
**Later evidence:** Ports, interviews, reviews, preservation wrappers, fan reconstructions, editors, expansions, Gold, and current stores are used only where labelled. They are not silently substituted for the three base releases.

## Source-quality key

- **Primary:** original manual or software documentation, direct developer statement, contemporary product page, or preserved period magazine page.
- **Near-primary:** direct contemporary player/developer forum discussion or institutional record closely tied to the object.
- **Secondary:** independent contemporary review, later critical reconstruction, interview, or historical analysis.
- **Tertiary:** database, search index, or bibliographic orientation; not sole support for a central mechanical claim.
- **Publisher/community claim:** useful evidence of intent, practice, custodianship, or perceived success, but potentially promotional, anecdotal, selected, or survivor-biased.

The three manuals were downloaded as PDFs and converted to searchable text. Every page was also rendered to an image; covers, contents, credits, setup, army-management, campaign, difficulty, multiplayer, and other key rules pages were visually inspected. The *Panzer General* and *Fantasy General* text layers contain occasional table and decorative-heading errors, so the rendered pages control. The 2012 *Panzer Corps* manual is clean and searchable but may describe a patched or clarified state rather than exact launch version 1.0.

The relevant pages of *Computer Gaming World* issues 122 and 143 were rendered and visually inspected. OCR was used for navigation and transcription only. The first article is a preview, so claims about absent features were checked against the final manual.

## Original manuals and mechanical evidence

### S01 — *Panzer General* DOS manual

- Type: original release manual; **primary**.
- URLs: [Datassette object page](https://datassette.org/manuais/us-estados-unidos-jogos-ibm-pc-manuais/panzer-general); [manual PDF](https://datassette.s3.us-west-004.backblazeb2.com/manuais/panzer_general.pdf)
- Used for: DOS identity; final credits; tutorial in the Poland scenario; tactical and strategic interfaces; ground/air stacking; move and attack order; objectives and capturing classes; core and auxiliary units; five campaign entry points; prestige; ordinary and elite replacements; the printed rule for automatic replacement between campaign scenarios; experience, overstrength, naming, and records; unit classes; initiative, entrenchment, rugged defence, suppression, support fire, terrain, weather, zones of control, spotting, fuel and ammunition; local rather than networked supply; aircraft fuel loss; difficulty and Custom controls; scenario/campaign side boundaries; hot-seat play; final-release play-by-mail save exchange; and absence of a base editor.
- Important corrections and discrepancy: the final manual includes PBM save files transferable by email, floppy, or modem, despite the preview saying that no such feature was planned. It says understrength core units are restored between scenarios with ordinary “replacements,” which begin at zero experience and dilute the unit. Player reports from 1997 and 2011 say that at least some program versions actually preserved experience; see S16 and S25.
- Caveat: rules prose sometimes says an AI control can be “adjusted” without explaining distinct decision methods. No search, planning, or learning architecture is disclosed. Unit-data tables are dense and were not exhaustively transcribed.

### S02 — *Fantasy General* DOS manual

- Type: original manual distributed with the current re-release; **primary for the 1996 base game**.
- URL: [manual PDF](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1741120/manuals/Fantasy_General_Manual_English.pdf?t=1728487091)
- Used for: complete credits and direct staff continuity; setting and five-continent campaign; four commanders and their special rules; first-battle tutorial; continent-map choice; army roster; ten classes and four races; class research through percentage allocation; gold; grades and upgrades; magic items; heroes; melee, skirmish, missile, and siege attacks; physical, magical, and mechanical attack media; wounds, kills, Rest, Recruit, morale, retreat, surrender, encirclement, flanking, spells, flying, terrain, zones of control, and spotting; Campaign, Scenario, and Arena modes; random Arena maps; custom armies and commanders; saved/imported armies; PBM file procedure; difficulty; and the explicit definition of Arena “AI Level” as purchased-unit experience.
- Important correction: Campaign difficulty adjusts enemy gold and purchased-unit experience. The Arena's “AI Level” also means starting unit experience. These labels do not establish several smarter reasoning systems.
- Caveat: the current Steam-hosted PDF is a preservation copy of the original manual, not proof that the modern wrapper reproduces every historical peripheral or multiplayer workflow unchanged. Illustrated unit tables required visual checking.

### S03 — *Panzer Corps* manual, 2012 edition

- Type: official base-game manual; **primary for mature base rules / near-primary for exact 2011 launch state**.
- URLs: [Steam-hosted manual](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/268400/manuals/SLIT_PZC_Localization_UK_Materials_Manual.pdf?t=1726821731); [Slitherine-hosted manual](https://www.slitherine.com/files/panzer_corps/Manual/Panzer-Corps-Manual-English.pdf)
- Used for: Lordz/Matrix/Slitherine credits; Alex Shargin's main-design role; five campaign starts; separate tutorial campaign; Axis/Allied/hot-seat scenario choice; five named difficulty levels; weather, supply, and fog options; contextual tutorial messages; interface and map controls; core/auxiliary borders and reserve list; campaign deployment; manual green versus elite replacement; prestige, experience, overstrength, upgrades, medals, heroes, and bonus SS units; unit classes; mass attack; close defence; initiative, entrenchment, rugged defence and displayed probability; support fire; unit switching; extended combat prediction; conditional undo; PBEM++; editor; and exposed modding of scenarios, equipment, campaigns, rules, and UI.
- Important boundary: copyright is 2012, one year after release. It is treated as an official mature base manual, not a byte-for-byte record of version 1.0.
- Caveat: the five difficulty labels are listed but their internal behavioural and resource changes are not disclosed. Promotional claims for PBEM++ and the editor are checked against independent reviews where possible.

## *Panzer General*: development, reception, and history

### S04 — Alan Emrich, “Rock 'n Roll Panzer Pushing,” *Computer Gaming World* 122, September 1994

- Type: independent contemporary preview based on a pre-release build; **secondary for evaluation / near-primary observation of SSI development culture**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_122.pdf)
- Used for: direct observation of SSI staff playing the Japanese Mega Drive/Genesis *Daisenryaku* using a crude translation; that game's addictive, fast, and fun qualities as inspiration; the design's optional statistical depth; preview scenario range; interface and display options; elastic scale; one ground/sea plus one air unit per hex; movement and attack in either order; initiative; local supply; combined-arms order; persistent campaign units; experience dilution; prestige as perhaps the preview's best feature; and Emrich's personal “truly addicting” judgement despite limited historical insight.
- Important boundary: this is not the final review. The build lacked some eventual content, medals, PBM, Allied campaigns, and an editor. The final manual establishes that PBM was added; no Allied campaign or editor appears in the base release.
- Caveat: one enthusiastic expert preview. Expected final content and quality are predictions, not release facts. Its scale criticism and grognard-accessibility tension remain evaluative.

### S05 — Jimmy Maher, “Opening the Gold Box, Part 7: Back to the Roots,” *The Digital Antiquarian* (2020)

- Type: later archival history and critical reconstruction; **secondary**.
- URL: [article](https://www.filfre.net/2020/11/opening-the-gold-box-part-7-back-to-the-roots/)
- Used for: Paul Murray's initial experiment; the SSI all-staff labour-of-love context; *Daisenryaku* influence; multimedia and accessibility intent; reported development cost; scenario/campaign structure; larger campaign-tree size; successful and losing branches; potential negative feedback; limited historical and supply modelling; fast interface; newsreel and animation presentation; unit naming, records, experience, custom composition, and emotional attachment; the five-star final *Computer Gaming World* reception; reported first-fifteen-month sales above 200,000 and broader SSI success; declining Five-Star sales after the original; one explicit rejection of playing the Nazi role; and lack of a commercial digital re-release as of 2020.
- Caveat: the article synthesises interviews, Strong Museum archival material, magazines, and the author's criticism. Sales and “most successful” language are historian/interview-derived rather than audited public accounts. The ethical objection is the author's position, not a player survey.

### S06 — “Interrogation of Joel Billings,” *A Wargamer's Needful Things* (2016)

- Type: direct creator/executive interview; **primary for Billings's recollection / secondary for broad history**.
- URL: [interview](https://www.awargamersneedfulthings.co.uk/2016/08/interrogation-of-joel-billings.html)
- Used for: Billings's view of *Panzer General* as a special SSI project; its role in introducing people to computer wargaming; the collective veteran-team character of development; and the later continuity he perceived between *Panzer General* and *Panzer Corps*.
- Caveat: retrospective recollection more than twenty years after the original. Praise and influence claims reflect the interviewee's perspective.

### S07 — Joel Billings on the *Panzer General* credits and voice work, Matrix forum (2022)

- Type: direct creator statement in a public forum; **primary for recollection**.
- URL: [forum thread](https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?t=388553)
- Used for: Billings's statement that he designed the campaign, wrote the voice script, and participated in the voice production; identification of Barry Lank as the voice performer; and his description of the game as a labour of love made by a “who's who” of SSI staff.
- Caveat: informal recollection and not a substitute for the printed credit list. The manual controls formal roles.

### S08 — *Panzer General* award and review bibliographic record

- Type: period-issue locator plus historical game database; **tertiary orientation / primary issue record not rendered in this pass**.
- URLs: [CGW issue 130 gallery](https://cgwmuseum.org/galleries/index.php?id=130&pub=2&year=1995); [MobyGames record](https://www.mobygames.com/game/1021/panzer-general/)
- Used for: orientation to *Computer Gaming World*'s 1994 Wargame of the Year award, PC Gamer US's award, and the later reputation of the original.
- Caveat: the award pages and final five-star review were not individually rendered here. The dossier does not use this source for mechanics or detailed review wording. The stronger Digital Antiquarian archive synthesis corroborates the final five-star reception.

## *Fantasy General*: contemporary and later reception

### S09 — Elliott Chin, “PanzerArmee Fantasy,” *Computer Gaming World* 143, June 1996

- Type: independent contemporary DOS review; **secondary**.
- URL: [issue PDF](https://www.cgwmuseum.org/galleries/issues/cgw_143.pdf)
- Used for: five-star verdict; description as the true sequel and equal of *Panzer General*; praise for pacing, introductory first continent, learning curve, interface, AI target selection and rear attacks, turn-limit pressure, unit catalogue, research, experience, performance on modest hardware, and estimated campaign scale; plus criticism of shallow magic, missing pre-commitment scenario descriptions, sharp difficulty rise, and Windows 95 crashes.
- Caveat: one exceptionally favourable review. The review's “well over 100 hours” statement is not telemetry and may partly reproduce publisher positioning. Its behavioural AI observations are valuable, but “great AI” is not an implementation audit.

### S10 — Trent Ward, *Fantasy General* review, GameSpot

- Type: preserved adverse review; **secondary**.
- URL: [review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/fantasy-general-review/1900-2535955/)
- Used for: the 4.9 verdict; criticism that the game is a *Panzer General* reskin, has a generic plot, does not satisfy an obvious wargame/RPG audience, ends the campaign after one lost battle, and gives a weak static presentation after continent victory.
- Date caveat: the surviving article page says 1 May 2000, while GameSpot's game/review index reports 1 May 1996. The criticism is retained without calling it securely contemporary to launch.
- Caveat: extremely hostile and brief relative to the system's breadth. It is most useful as a counterweight to the five-star *Computer Gaming World* response, not as a neutral mechanical inventory.

### S11 — Andy Butcher, *Fantasy General* review, *Arcane* 6, May 1996

- Type: contemporary independent review located through a hosted issue and bibliographic transcription; **secondary, incompletely inspected**.
- URLs: [hosted issue](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/63239317/arcane-magazine-may-1996-6); [bibliographic orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_General)
- Used for: 7/10 rating and the specific adverse retention judgement that the game could occupy several pleasant hours without necessarily provoking a late-night “one more go” response.
- Caveat: the exact page was not independently extracted from the hosted flipbook in this pass; the wording survives through bibliographic transcription. It supports rather than anchors the retention analysis.

### S12 — *Fantasy General*, GamesFirst archive (7 October 1996)

- Type: independent contemporary review; **secondary**.
- URL: [review](https://gamesfirst.com/articles/1996/fantasy-general/)
- Used for: an accessible description of controls and battle flow; praise for presentation and music; and one reviewer's testimony that the game appealed despite his usual lack of enthusiasm for strategy titles.
- Caveat: one favourable anecdote and a four-out-of-five editorial rating. It does not measure genre conversion or long-term return.

## *Panzer Corps*: product, development, reception, and community

### S15 — Official *Panzer Corps: Wehrmacht* product page

- Type: official publisher catalogue; **primary for product scope / publisher claim for quality**.
- URL: [Slitherine product page](https://www.slitherine.com/product/panzer-corps)
- Used for: base twenty-six-scenario campaign tree; core-force continuity; 1–2 players; AI; PBEM++; editor; publisher championship/news record; discontinuation of the standalone product after 6 February 2020; and the publisher's explicit positioning as a classic design brought to modern standards.
- Caveat: the page was updated across the product's life and contains figures associated with the mature ecosystem. It is not used to attribute later 800-unit or DLC-scale content to the 2011 base game. Promotional quality language is not independent reception.

### S16 — Matrix launch discussion, “Reviews and Impressions?” (July 2011)

- Type: near-contemporary player/developer forum discussion; **near-primary for launch practice and direct publisher statements**.
- URL: [thread, page 2](https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?start=20&t=207070)
- Used for: Iain McNeil's statement that Shargin's *Panzer General Forever* was the foundation of *Panzer Corps* before the commercial team and reskin; developer explanation of replacing automatic between-scenario repair; the free-green/paid-elite controversy; exposed `gamerules` data; requests for bulk repair controls; praise for the tutorial and larger manoeuvre space; and immediate “puzzle-like” player framing.
- Important discrepancy: several participants remember original *Panzer General* as granting free elite repair. Its manual says ordinary replacements, which dilute experience, but similar 1997 program reports predate this launch debate. The conflict is retained rather than treating either the later memory or printed manual as a complete account.
- Caveat: self-selected launch enthusiasts in an official publisher forum. Developer statements are strong evidence of intent; player approval and complaint frequencies are not representative.

### S17 — Tim Stone, *Panzer Corps* review, PC Gamer (9 October 2011)

- Type: independent contemporary review; **secondary**.

### S19 — *Panzer Corps* review, Digitally Downloaded (12 July 2011)

### S20 — “AI and difficulty: a friendly critique,” Slitherine forum (July 2011)

- Type: detailed launch-player critique with direct lead-designer response; **near-primary for observed launch behaviour and developer acknowledgement**.

## Cross-lineage rule discrepancy

### S25 — 1997 Usenet discussion of *Panzer General* between-scenario replacements

- Type: near-contemporary player reports; **near-primary community testimony**.
- URL: [comp.sys.ibm.pc.games.strategic thread](https://groups.google.com/g/comp.sys.ibm.pc.games.strategic/c/--ZkJVyNs3g)
- Used for: two players' direct correction of another poster's manual-consistent description, reporting that their copies supplied elite or experience-preserving replacements between scenarios.
- Caveat: the exact platform build, patch level, and observation method are not recorded. Two users do not prove behaviour in every DOS release, but the 1997 date makes the reports more than a 2011 memory and establishes a real documentation/program discrepancy worth retaining.

## Known but not fully examined

- *Computer Gaming World* issue 126 contains the final *Panzer General* review and issue 130 the award record, but those pages were not separately rendered. The preview, final manual, and later archive-based history were sufficient for the principal design findings.
- Period 3DO, PlayStation, Macintosh, and Windows conversion reviews were located but excluded because Barry did not identify a port and platform conflation would add more noise than evidence.
- *Allied General*, *Pacific General*, *Star General*, *Panzer General II*, and the later sequel branch were not mechanically reconstructed. Their presence is used only to establish the lineage around the three requested objects.
- *Fantasy General*'s *Island Challenge* content was not reconstructed. Current stores prove inclusion, not base-campaign mechanics.
- The exact original *Arcane* page was not extracted from its flipbook host. The rating and retention sentence are used only as supporting adverse testimony.
- Contemporary *Next Generation* and later GameFAQs responses to *Fantasy General* were located but add little beyond the already well-documented split between accessibility, shallowness, and attachment.
- No audited sales ledger for any of the three games was found. *Panzer General* estimates are archival/interview-derived; current publisher “millions” language for Gold is not adopted.
- No source code, AI technical design, controlled scenario corpus, opponent win rates, player telemetry, completion rate, session-length distribution, or longitudinal retention dataset was found.
- No known-version executable test was conducted to resolve the *Panzer General* between-scenario replacement discrepancy. The manual and player-reported program behaviour remain separate evidence.
- The current legal availability of the 1994 original remains awkward. The absence of a commercial re-release is a negative search finding supported by the 2020 history, not proof that no obscure licensed distribution exists anywhere.
- No attempt was made to inventory every *Panzer Corps* patch, platform port, DLC scenario, community mod, tournament result, editor guide, or PBEM challenge.
- Current store review aggregates are preserved as dated observations and should be refreshed if used in the later audit.
