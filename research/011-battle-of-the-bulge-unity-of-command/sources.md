# Sources: *Battle of the Bulge* and *Unity of Command*

Accessed 23 August 2026 unless otherwise stated. Source IDs are used in the accompanying dossier. The ledger distinguishes the original 2012 iPad *Battle of the Bulge* from its 2015 multi-platform revision, and the 2011 base *Unity of Command: Stalingrad Campaign* from later patches, editor support, DLC, and sequel.

## *Battle of the Bulge*: rules and first-party evidence

### S01 — Shenandoah Studio / Slitherine, *Battle of the Bulge Game Manual* (2015 PC adaptation)

- Type: official manual for the revised multi-platform release; **primary for the mature rules, not automatically for 2012 launch content**.
- URL: [Steam-hosted PDF](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/365560/manuals/Battle_of_the_Bulge_manual_EBOOK.pdf?t=1726823784).
- Used for: scale; sequence of play; initiative; one-space activation; passage-of-time probabilities; December 16 surprise restrictions; control; movement, roads, rivers, and stacking; combat participation and modifiers; terrain absorption; retreat and breakthrough; supply, isolation, air supply, fuel shortage, reinforcements, replacements, weather, events, scoring, and moving victory thresholds; and the names of the original iOS team.
- Edition boundary: this is a September 2015 manual produced for the PC/Mac adaptation. It contains *Endgame*, *Luck of the Draw*, and *Bitter End* variants and later platform material not present in the December 2012 launch package. Core rules are corroborated against launch reviews, the 2013 Player's Guide, and the later physical adaptation before being described as the original design.
- Credit boundary: the manual lists the iOS team collectively rather than assigning every role. It must not be used to infer that every listed person designed the rules or AI.

### S02 — Shenandoah Studio launch release (10 December 2012)

- Type: publisher press release preserved by There Will Be Games; **primary for launch identity and publisher claims**.
- URL: [“Shenandoah Studio Releases ‘Battle of the Bulge,’ First Serious Wargame on iPad”](https://therewillbe.games/news-press-releases/3717-shenandoah-studio-releases-qbattle-of-the-bulge-first-serious-wargame-on-ipad).
- Used for: 13 December 2012 iPad/iPad Mini release; $9.99 launch price; two launch scenarios; tutorial, full rules, historical commentary, and asynchronous Game Center play; May 2012 crowdfunding; and the claims that about 1,100 people backed the project and raised roughly twice the requested amount.
- Caveat: ease, strategic richness, and careful-planning language are promotional claims. The release's “half an hour” short-scenario estimate is retained alongside faster reviewer estimates rather than treated as measured duration.

### S03 — Kickstarter campaign and FAQ (April–May 2012)

- Type: first-party crowdfunding record; **primary for intended platform and campaign framing**.
- URLs: [project FAQ](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ericleesmith/battle-of-the-bulge-the-simulation-game-for-the-ip/faqs); [contemporary Pocket Gamer campaign report](https://www.pocketgamer.com/battle-of-the-bulge/historical-ipad-strategy-game-battle-of-the-bulge-advances-on-kickstarter/).
- Used for: $20,000 funding target; initial concentration on iPad performance and interface; intended later iPhone support; and the social/asynchronous-play emphasis during development.
- Access caveat: Kickstarter returned a 403 to direct page inspection in the research environment, although its indexed FAQ text was visible. Final funding and backer counts therefore come from S02 and the independent contemporary report in S06, not an uninspected campaign total.

### S04 — Shenandoah Studio, *Battle of the Bulge Player's Guide*, v1.2a (2013)

- Type: publisher-produced collection of community strategy essays with staff prefaces; **primary for the surviving original-iOS interface and near-primary/community evidence for developed play**.
- URL: [preserved PDF](https://www.histogames.com/images/news/octobre2013/032/Bulge-Players-Guide-Small.pdf).
- Used for: the original map's 61 individually shaped areas; calendar awareness; road-network commitment; river assaults and cross-day follow-ups; the three-unit stack limit; the combat forecast; pre-commit undo; Game Center workflow; the short scenario's different priorities; and evidence of an active strategy-writing community within a year of release.
- Caveat: most chapters are player essays, not official rules or representative usage data. Strategy claims demonstrate experienced interpretation, not universal optimality or balance. The Histogames URL was indexed with the complete 28-page PDF and remained readable through the research environment, but a direct live request returned `404` on 2026-08-23; treat it as a preservation pointer rather than guaranteed live hosting.

### S05 — Miguel A. Nieves, “Artificial Intelligence: Utility builds Character” (12 December 2012)

- Type: direct technical account by the original AI programmer; **primary for launch AI architecture and design intent**.
- URL: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/artificial-intelligence-utility-builds-character).
- Used for: utility normalisation; externalised archetypes; the Control Yoke, Strategic Brain, and Tactical Nodes; the hierarchical finite-state control loop; modelling the AI as another interface-using player; AI-versus-AI testing; staged development from legal random moves to strategic archetypes; faction-specific weighted criteria; launch inventory of eight agents; lack of a deep game-tree search; and the advantages and tuning costs Nieves reported.
- Critical boundary: an architecture account proves how the AI was intended and implemented, not how strongly it played. Independent play evidence in S06–S08 is kept separate and conflicting.

### S06 — David Dunham interview with Matt Thrower (11 February 2014)

- Type: direct developer retrospective; **primary for platform, simulation, interface, and AI intent**.
- URL: [Pocket Gamer interview](https://www.pocketgamer.com/drive-on-moscow/shenandoah-studios-david-dunham-on-why-hed-love-to-get-more-people-playing-games/).
- Used for: explicit iPad-first design; whole-map fit, low piece count, and short discrete turns as platform requirements; serious historical play combined with computer-game polish; simulation as recreating choices rather than reproducing events; supply as a major historical concern; data-driven utility personalities; and the admission that larger games amplify utility-AI weaknesses.
- Caveat: Dunham's statement that the approach worked “pretty well” in *Bulge* is a developer assessment. It does not override S07's sustained adverse AI account.

## *Battle of the Bulge*: contemporary reception and developed play

### S07 — Bruce Geryk, “John Butterfield's *Battle of the Bulge* rejuvenates an old rogue” (28 December 2012)

- Type: detailed contemporary review after several dozen reported games; **secondary, high-value for repeated-play observation**.
- URL: [Quarter to Three](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2012/12/28/john-butterfields-battle-of-the-bulge-rejuvenates-an-old-rogue/).
- Used for: tablet-native presentation; single-area activation; variable impulse clock; ruthless tuning of a small map and unit set; moving victory thresholds; random but memorable turning points; historical-strength compromises; strong two-human play; and the reviewer's detailed criticism of exploitable, weak original AI—especially on the Allied counteroffensive.
- Caveat: one highly experienced critic's account, not telemetry. Its strong AI verdict conflicts with S08 and with the developer's assessment in S06; the conflict is retained rather than averaged.

### S08 — Michael Barnes, “Cracked LCD—*Battle of the Bulge* (iOS) in Review” (20 December 2012)

- Type: enthusiastic launch review after several days of intensive play; **secondary**.
- URL: [No High Scores](https://www.nohighscores.com/2012/12/20/cracked-lcd-battle-of-the-bulge-ios-in-review/).
- Used for: basics slides, tutorial, transparent rulebook and tables; approximately fifteen-minute short games; two scenarios and two opposing personalities per side/scenario choice; terrain-hit animation, forecasts, and undo; variable zero-to-120-minute advances; stack coordination; async/hot-seat support; “addictive” time pressure; and a favourable early assessment of AI challenge.
- Caveat: the play window was short and enthusiasm unusually high. Expected future multiplayer activity is not observed retention.

### S09 — Tof Eklund, “*Battle of the Bulge* for iPad Review—A Grognard's Wardream” (14 January 2013)

- Type: contemporary review; **secondary**.
- URL: [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2013/01/14/battle-of-the-bulge-review/).
- Used for: five-star reception; accessible Risk-like control with deeper area, terrain, road, bridge, supply, and historical-unit structure; polished audiovisual/historical presentation; two launch scenarios; multiplayer as the reviewer's replay core; and limited single-player content as the principal adverse finding.
- Chronology caveat: the review predicted that an iPhone Universal version was unlikely; version 2.0 made the app Universal in October 2013. That forecast is not repeated as an enduring limitation.

### S10 — Shenandoah Studio, “The ‘Battle’ Just Got a Little Smaller” (18 October 2013)

- Type: publisher update release; **primary for post-launch version 2.0, peripheral to the original**.
- URL: [PRWeb preservation](https://www.prweb.com/releases/the_battle_just_got_a_little_smaller_battle_of_the_bulge_is_now_universal/prweb11237790.htm).
- Used for: date and scope of the later Universal iPhone/iPod/iPad update; Spanish and Japanese localisation; and in-app availability of the Player's and Designer's Guides.
- Boundary: these are afterlife additions, not launch features.

## *Unity of Command*: rules and first-party evidence

### S17 — 2x2 Games, *Unity of Command: Stalingrad Campaign Game Manual* (copyright 2011; compiled PDF 2013)

- Type: official manual; **primary for the original core rules with an explicit later-content boundary**.
- URL: [Steam-hosted PDF](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/218090/manuals/UoC%20Manual.pdf?t=1726129725).
- Used for: 20-km hexes and four-day turns; IGOUGO phases; objectives and victory timing; campaign branching and prestige; unit steps, suppression, experience, entrenchment, movement, ZOC, dynamic front lines, combat odds, retreat, overrun, supply, reinforcements, specialists, theatre assets, History Mode, and interface information.
- Version boundary: the PDF visibly boxes later “Rules version 1.1—applies in DLC and community scenarios” material and includes the editor added around version 1.04. Those items are not attributed to the 15 November 2011 launch. Rules outside the boxes are treated as the mature base system where contemporary reviews corroborate them.

### S18 — Matrix Games product and release record

- Type: publisher/store record with archived installer/update dates; **primary for commercial identity, near-primary for feature framing**.
- URL: [Matrix Games](https://www.matrixgames.com/game/unity-of-command/pc).

### S19 — 2x2 Games official site and original product page

- Type: developer site preserved in its later first-game/DLC state; **primary for official scope and feature count**.
- URLs: [official game page](https://unityofcommand.net/); [older home page](https://unityofcommand.net/home/).
- Used for: 2011 origin; base *Stalingrad Campaign* boundary; official count of seventeen scenarios; 20-km/four-day scale; History Mode; and separation of later *Red Turn* and *Black Turn* content.

### S20 — 2x2 Games, “The Power of Supply” (3 November 2011)

- Type: pre-release developer article; **primary for supply-system intent**.
- URL: [Unity of Command developer blog](https://unityofcommand.net/blog/2011/11/03/the-power-of-supply/).
- Used for: the intended importance, visibility, and operational consequences of the supply network; rail and range behaviour; and encirclement/breakout framing.
- Access caveat: the live server returned 403 to direct inspection in the research environment. Central mechanical claims are therefore anchored to the accessible official manual (S17), while this entry supports contemporaneous design provenance only.

### S21 — Jessica Conditt interview with Tomislav Uzelac and Nenad Jalšovec (24 December 2011)

- Type: direct launch-period developer interview; **primary for origin and design intent**.
- URL: [Joystiq preservation at Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-12-24-the-joystiq-indie-pitch-unity-of-command.html).
- Used for: Panzer General inspiration; ambition to capture traditional hex-wargame essentials without excessive complexity; simple historical rules; striking supply lines as often as formations; historical orders of battle and scenario challenges; information-accessibility goals; small independent Zagreb team; and colourful but non-technical claims about aggressive AI.
- Caveat: “criminal personality” AI language is humorous promotional testimony, not an architecture disclosure or benchmark.

### S22 — Tomislav Uzelac, 2014 answers published by Spillhistorie (2020)

- Type: direct designer interview; **primary for design philosophy**.
- URL: [Spillhistorie](https://spillhistorie.no/2020/08/28/a-few-words-with-tomislav-uzelac-from-2x2-games/).
- Used for: “textbook Blitzkrieg” as an intended learned behaviour; rejection of mechanics that do not represent well in the UI; deriving maximum leverage from a small rule set; supply and suppression as proxies for omitted explicit morale/flanking; and the computer as a rules-handling “staff officer.”
- Dating caveat: answers were written in 2014 and published in 2020. They are retrospective explanation, not a launch document.

### S23 — 2x2 retrospective development diaries for *Unity of Command II* (2016)

- Type: direct sequel-development retrospectives; **primary for the developers' later assessment, peripheral to original mechanics**.
- URLs: [“Development Diary 5: The Supply Network”](https://unityofcommand.net/blog/2016/04/06/development-diary-5-the-supply-network/); [“Developer Diary 8: Campaign”](https://unityofcommand.net/blog/2016/07/26/developer-diary-8-campaign/).
- Used for: confirmation that the original aimed to make supply as graspable and important as frontline tactics; admission that scenario designers sometimes expanded supply ranges to offset bad weather and thereby weakened intended weather/logistics interaction; and the later description of the first game's campaign as “somewhat bare-bones.”
- Boundary: sequel solutions and mechanics are excluded. Only explicit retrospective claims about the first game are retained.

## *Unity of Command*: contemporary reception and repeated-play evidence

### S24 — Tim Stone, *Unity of Command* review (5 March 2012)

- Type: contemporary professional review; **secondary**.
- URL: [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/unity-of-command-review/).
- Used for: 86/100 reception; petite but historically flavoured battles; supply encirclement and suffocation; praised AI behaviour including withdrawal, weak-point selection, and supply cutting; two campaigns; and the campaign's light prestige continuity.
- Counting caveat: the review refers to eighteen scenarios, while the official site says seventeen and other records say “17+.” The discrepancy is reported rather than silently resolved.

### S25 — Richard Lane, *Unity of Command* review (9 November 2011)

- Type: pre-release/contemporary professional review; **secondary**.
- URL: [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/unity-of-command/review).
- Used for: easy learning and willingness to teach; tutorial and introductory scenarios; small forces around twenty formations; typical nine-to-ten-turn, thirty-to-sixty-minute scenarios; objective-clock tension; AI use of flanks, supply cuts, and theatre assets; and control frictions including accidental movement, absent undo, small symbols, and underexplained deeper detail.
- Accuracy caveat: the review incorrectly labels the campaign Operation Barbarossa. It is not used for setting chronology. Its language of multiple “attack points” is also not a substitute for the manual's one-AP base rule.

### S26 — Hentzau, “Thoughts: *Unity of Command*” (28 February 2012)

- Type: disclosed beta-tester/review-copy account after intensive play; **secondary, high-value adverse evidence**.
- URL: [The Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-unity-of-command/).
- Used for: tanks as breakthrough tools, infantry as line-holders and suppression exploiters; supply/time pressure forcing envelopment; brutal AI; fixed setups producing a “war-themed puzzle” experience; random overruns dominating at least one small scenario; repeated restarts; perceived low replay after solving; light/linear campaign; and a positive overall verdict despite those reservations.
- Caveat: one experienced critic with a free copy; scenario counts are approximate. The twenty-first-retry example is vivid but not a frequency estimate.

### S27 — JC, *Unity of Command* review (16 December 2011)

- Type: detailed contemporary enthusiast review and AAR author; **secondary**.
- URL: [Real and Simulated Wars](https://kriegsimulation.blogspot.com/2011/12/unity-of-command-review.html).
- Used for: official-like seventeen-scenario count; branch structure and prestige-only continuity; single-formation attacks rather than multi-hex combined attacks; minimal interface; strong offensive and defensive AI; supply-line targeting; no fog of war; and the game's middle position between superficial simplicity and monster simulation.
- Accuracy caveat: the review says a turn represents two weeks; the official manual says four days. The review's claim that developers omitted fog because the AI could not handle uncertainty is reported only as the reviewer's account, not established primary fact.

### S28 — Bruce Geryk, “*Unity of Command*: supply goes awry” (29 March 2012)

## Evidence excluded or kept peripheral

- No sequel mechanic is treated as part of either original. *Drive on Moscow*, *Desert Fox*, the 2015 revised *Bulge*, *Red Turn*, *Black Turn*, and *Unity of Command II* appear only for labelled afterlife or retrospective evidence.
- No current Steam review excerpt is used as direct evidence about the 2012 iPad AI. The Steam *Bulge* explicitly uses rewritten AI.
- No unverified current App Store availability, Game Center population, PBEM population, completion rate, or active-player count is claimed.
- Search snippets, Wikipedia, and copied store descriptions were used to locate stronger records, not for central rules where manuals or direct accounts existed.
- No architecture claim is made for *Unity of Command*'s AI beyond the credited programmer and observable behaviour. No source code, technical talk, search-depth account, or benchmark was located.
- The label “puzzle” is retained as a disputed player experience and a current Steam tag, not imposed as a genre verdict.
- Favourable and adverse AI accounts are not averaged. They differ in game, side, scenario, skill, play duration, and version.

## Main uncertainties carried into the dossier

1. Exact launch-day *Unity of Command* scenario counting: official pages say seventeen, some reviewers eighteen, and later lists can include bonus/tutorial/PvP or updated scenarios differently.
2. How much of the 2015 *Battle of the Bulge* manual's mature wording exactly matches the 2012 launch rules, even though the central system is strongly corroborated.
3. The practical meaning of the original *Bulge* AI's “eight agents” versus reviewers' four visible personality choices across sides and scenarios.
4. Representative win rates, scenario restarts, completion, replay, abandonment, and return behaviour for either game.
5. Typical human-versus-human balance by side and scenario, distinct from AI difficulty.
6. Whether *Unity of Command*'s strongly praised supply-aware AI remained challenging after scenario mastery, and which behaviours were scripted, heuristic, searched, or scenario-authored.
7. Current operational state of original iOS multiplayer and availability to new purchasers.
8. The degree to which fixed setups and random combat generated durable variation versus a solvable/retry loop for different players.
